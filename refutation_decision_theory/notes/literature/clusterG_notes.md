## 1. harsanyi1955: CONFIRMED

JPE 63(4):309–321, Aug. 1955. DOI **10.1086/257678**.

The aggregation theorem is in **Section III**. Postulates a, b and c are on **p. 313**: social preferences satisfy Marschak's postulates, individual preferences satisfy them too, and Pareto indifference holds. Theorems I–IV follow on pp. 313–314. **Theorem V** (W is a weighted sum of individual utilities, W = Σ aᵢUᵢ) is on **p. 314**. Cite "§III, pp. 313–314" or "Theorem V, p. 314".

## 2. broome1991: CONFIRMED (location found; book not seen directly)

Basil Blackwell, Oxford, 1991, Economics and Philosophy series, ISBN 0-631-17199-1.

The discussion is in **ch. 5, "Expected Utility and Rationality"**:

- §5.3 "Individuation of outcomes", p. 95;
- §5.4 "Transitivity of preferences", p. 100. Unconstrained individuation empties transitivity on p. 102. The principle of individuation by justifiers is on p. 103: "Outcomes should be distinguished as different if and only if they differ in a way that makes it rational to have a preference between them";
- §5.5 "Rational requirements of indifference", p. 104. A justifier is "simply the opposite of a rational requirement of indifference" (p. 104);
- §5.6 "Individuation and the sure-thing principle", p. 107. This is the parallel point for the sure-thing principle.

Suggested citation: ch. 5, §§5.3–5.6, esp. pp. 100–107.

Caveats:

- The section start pages come from a table of contents seen only in search snippets.
- pp. 102–104 and the quotations come from Filipović.
- Do not confuse the book with Broome's separate essay "Rationality and the sure-thing principle", in Meeks (ed.), *Thoughtful Economic Man*, CUP 1991, pp. 74–102.

## 5. debreu1954: CONFIRMED, with a precise location added

Chapter XI of *Decision Processes*, **pp. 159–165**, checked in the scan. Chapter XII (Hausner) starts on p. 167. The SEP's "159–66" is loose.

**Yes, the lexicographic example is in this paper.** It is **footnote 1, printed on p. 164**, attached to the sentence on p. 159: "it is always possible to define on that set a real-valued order-preserving function … This is easily seen to be false." The footnote gives the lexicographic ordering of the plane and the standard proof: disjoint non-degenerate intervals [α(a,b₁), α(a,b₂)] give an injection of the reals into a countable set. Debreu 1954 is therefore the standard source, and no substitute is needed. Cite "p. 159 n. 1" (footnote text on p. 164).

## 6. lewis1979arrow: CONFIRMED (pages partly secondary)

Noûs 13(4):455–476, Nov. 1979. DOI **10.2307/2215339**. Crossref gives only the first page. The last page, 476, comes from secondary bibliographies.

The small miracle is introduced in the Nixon example on **p. 468**. That is world w₁, where the laws are "violated at w₁ in some simple, localized, inconspicuous way" just before the button press. The ranked similarity criteria (1)–(4) are on **p. 472**. Both pages come from Pruss's quotations, not from the original.

Terminology: Lewis does not say "divergence miracle" in this paper. That label is from later literature, so it should not be put in quotation marks as Lewis's term.
