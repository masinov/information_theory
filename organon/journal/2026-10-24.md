- **meta/Q-necessity analysed** (mec/Necessity, proposed). The core's necessity is conceptual and
  conditional: any registration, of any kind, satisfies D, R and C, because that follows from what
  aboutness is (P). Not claimed: that registration occurs, or that the conditions hold apart from it.
  The conditional is also metaphysical if P is correct; the conditions themselves are contingent, and
  their holding here cannot be coherently denied by anyone who asserts anything. Stroud's objection does
  not reach the conditional; it survives narrowed to P (mec/Obj-stroud, partial answer).
- **New friction mec/F-descriptive** (overreach in P): aboutness by description, of the future, and of
  mathematical objects seems to lack the dependence P requires. Options: restrict P to basic aboutness
  (Claude recommends) or keep it general. **Decision for the author.**

- **Q3 worked** (mec/Which-position, proposed, refining Lead-repertoire). Which way F is can be
  registered from actual contrasts, relative to the variety F actually has: which of R's actual
  conditions answers, where each condition gets its relevance to F by answering to an actual term of F
  somewhere. Limit: not against mere possibilities. Requires plurality of instances, reidentification
  of conditions, and, beyond the co-present case, retention (so Q3 depends on Q4). It separates the
  content of which-way registration from F-single. Precedent: Saussure's differential value. If
  adopted, F-which and the process frame's FP-which close by restriction; FP-math does not.
  **Decision for the author: adopt, and resolve F-which by restriction?**

- **The receiver need not distinguish** (author's questions). Recorded: mec/F-subjectR (the argument for
  R makes R distinguish, against E6), mec/F-pairing (term-by-term pairing in Df-matching is not argued
  for), and first the proposal mec/One-field (one field, target and receiver as parts of it), which
  misread the author and overreached (unity, parthood, a container given first); withdrawn. Replaced by
  the author's point, mec/Field-aspect: whatever is subject to differentiation is, insofar as it is, a
  field; the differentiation clause of R reduces to F's concept; R becomes a consequence of C plus
  distinct contrasts; Df-roles follows instead of being asserted. Pairing and reidentification then
  become explicit extra commitments of which-way registration (Which-position).
