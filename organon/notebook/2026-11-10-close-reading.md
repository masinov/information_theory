# Close reading of "A Minimal Epistemic Core" (state of commit 8968376)

Claude, 2026-11-10, at the author's request: "carefully analyze the text from start to finish, taking
notes of every statement made, how it unfolds from the previous one… Be very meticulous, slow,
careful." Nothing in the paper was changed while these notes were written.

Method. The whole source was read again from the first line, not from memory of the edits. For each
statement: what it says; what it is doing at that point; where it comes from in what precedes; whether
it holds. Verdicts: **holds**; **holds, wording** (true, badly put); **gap** (does not follow from what
is given); **conflict** (cannot stand with another passage, cited); **status** (true or false aside,
its kind is misstated: assumed, derived, stipulated). Line numbers are those of the source.

The seven errors found earlier the same day fall under four patterns, and they are the patterns
looked for here:
- (α) an assumption presented as a derivation;
- (β) a correction made in one place and not carried to the others;
- (γ) one word in two senses;
- (δ) the kind of an item (assumed, argued, stipulated, method) shifting between passages.

## A. Abstract (l.109–133)

**A1** (l.109–111). "The minimal structure of the basic form of any epistemic relation: the relation in
which something is perceived, or taken up in any way, prior to concepts and descriptions."
- Does: names the subject.
- "Taken up" is the paper's word for the subject before any theory, and it is never defined, listed as
  unanalysed, or listed as presupposed. It already means reception. So principle 2 (receptivity) is
  nearly contained in the description of the subject. That is acceptable only if said. **Status.**
- "Basic form": see P3 and E7. The phrase has two senses in the paper. **Conflict (γ).**

**A2** (l.111–112). The count. See §0.

**A3** (l.112–114). Determinacy, with "this does not reduce what a thing is to how it differs".
**Holds** as a report of §2.

**A4** (l.114–115). Receptivity. **Holds** as a report.

**A5** (l.115–118). "From these it follows that what such a relation is of is a difference; that the
registering is itself a difference…; and that both are differences between actual terms, since only
what holds can be that in virtue of which something holds."
- "It follows": three things are said to follow. The first needs the further step (P11), which the
  abstract has just called an assumption, so "from these" must include it. The second is not derived
  anywhere (P14). The third is not derived for the registering (P14) and is not validly derived for
  what is registered (P12, P16). **Gap (α)**, three times.
- The reason given, "only what holds can be that in virtue of which something holds", concerns what is
  depended upon. It says nothing about the registering, which is what depends.

**A6** (l.118–121). The three arguments about alternatives, compressed. The third clause now matches §2.
"Not preconditions of the relation" is stronger than what §2 shows (P16–P18). **Gap.**

**A7** (l.122–123). Registration "presupposes neither understanding, nor representation, nor order, nor
a pairing of terms, nor a knowing subject". True of the definition. **Holds.**

**A8** (l.123–125). Two unanalysed notions, bound by stated conditions. **Holds**, but see F3 and C5
for the conditions themselves.

**A9** (l.125–128). Formal; necessary, not sufficient; "the necessity of an analysis". See P19.

**A10** (l.128–130). Schema; two realizations show it is met. **Holds** (E2, E3).

**A11** (l.130–132). "The base of the epistemic is not itself epistemic… what makes a registration
epistemic lies in what is built on it." See E7: this cannot stand with "basic" as §2 defines it.
**Conflict (γ).**

**A12** (l.132–133). The paper lists everything it assumes. It does not: see K1.

## P. §2 The Guiding Premise (l.203–317)

**P1** (l.205–210). The premise.
- First sentence: "a receptive relation to something in particular". Two things are packed in:
  receptive (principle 2) and to something in particular (the starting assumption).
- P1: "What it is of is a difference: X as it differs, in some respect, from another **actual** term."
- P2: "That by which it is of that difference, the registering, is itself a difference, and it holds
  at least partly in virtue of the first."
- "That by which it is of that difference" is how "the registering" is introduced. It is obscure, and
  from here to l.317 "registering", "registers", "registered" are used in an untheoretical sense,
  although l.213–214 reserves "registration" for the definition of §7. **Conflict (γ, β).**

**P2** (l.212–216). "Basic" defined: "prior to concepts and descriptions: the relation in which
something is perceived or taken up". Converse of the premise "is false".
- This fixes sense (a) of "basic": a kind of epistemic relation, perception as against judgement.

**P3** (l.218–220). The count: a starting assumption, two principles, one further step.

**P4** (l.222–223). Determinacy, first sentence: "Whatever is some way in particular differs: there is
a way that it is not, and its being this way in particular, as against any other, lies in that."
- Two claims. (a) If a thing is some way in particular, there is a way it is not. (b) Its
  particularity lies in that.
- (a) quantifies over ways, including ways nothing is ("actual or not", l.232). With such ways
  allowed, (a) is close to trivial: every way has another. The substantive claim is (b).
- **Holds**, with that understood.

**P5** (l.223–225). Not a reduction. **Holds**; it is the correction made after the author's question.

**P6** (l.225–227). "What is said concerns the particularity of the way: it does not go without a
difference, **and a difference is what a relation to the thing in particular has to answer to**."
- The second half is the "further step" of P11. Here it is asserted inside the statement of
  determinacy. At l.252 the same thing is said not to follow from the two principles.
- **Conflict (β).** The correction of l.252 was added without removing its earlier appearance.

**P7** (l.227–228). "A way that excluded nothing would mark nothing, and a thing that was no way in
particular could not be what anything is of."
- The second clause is a separate premise: it carries the passage from "to something in particular"
  to "to something that is some way in particular" (used at l.250–251). It sits here as if it were a
  reason for determinacy. **Status (δ)**: an unlisted premise.

**P8** (l.228–232). Not derived; "applied without exception: to what a relation is of, to the
registering, and to the conditions of whatever receives". The last two are not different things: the
registering is a contrast between conditions. Point of refusal stated. **Holds, wording.**

**P9** (l.234–236). Receptivity: "what is taken up is that to which the taking-up is due. A registering
holds, at least partly, in virtue of what it registers. This principle marks out which relation is
being analysed."
- States an identity: what is taken up = what the taking-up is due to. That gives both directions:
  whatever is taken up is depended on, and whatever is depended on is taken up. The second direction
  is the indiscriminateness of §5 (l.562–567). Consistent.
- "Due" and "in virtue of" are used as one notion. At l.477–479 the paper says "due to" is "the
  ordinary word" and "carries none of these conditions". But here "due to" states the principle, and
  at l.252 a condition is asserted of it ("what something is due to must itself hold").
  **Conflict (γ).**
- "Marks out which relation is being analysed": receptivity is then partly a delimitation of the
  subject. Compatible with P2 sense (a) only if every perception is receptive, which is assumed.

**P10** (l.236–248). What falls short.
- l.237–239: likeness, correspondence, ordering. "Each of these relations holds between the two by how
  each of them is." True of likeness and correspondence. False of ordering: that one thing is before
  or above another is not settled by how each is. The intended point is that in none of them does the
  one contribute to the other's holding. **Holds, wording** (the stated reason is wrong for one case).
- l.239–241: the consequences if they sufficed. Elucidation, not refutation; the paper says "content
  is shown". **Holds.**
- l.241–246: agreement by arrangement. "Whatever aboutness there is in the case is owed to a
  dependence somewhere in it." "Aboutness" appears here for the first time, undefined. In the
  "fitted" case there is a chain from K through the fitting to K′; whether K′ then holds in virtue of
  K turns on transitivity, which §5 leaves open. The text claims nothing that needs it. **Holds.**
- l.246–248: point of refusal; "a change of subject". **Holds** as a statement of position.

**P11** (l.250–257). The derivation, first half.
- "What a basic epistemic relation is of is something in particular, and so some way in particular."
  Uses the premise hidden in P7. **Status.**
- "By determinacy, it differs in some respect." From (a) of P4: there is a way it is not. This is not
  yet "differs from another term". **Holds** so far.
- "By receptivity, the registering is due to what it is of; and what something is due to must itself
  hold." The second clause is the first binding condition of "in virtue of" (l.475), used before it
  is stated, and of "due to". **Holds**, order aside.
- "One further step… to take something up in particular is to take it up as it differs."
  - The reason offered: "a registering due to a thing, but not to what makes its way a particular
    one, would be a taking-up of something that happens to be some way, **not of its being that
    way**." This does not follow once P5 is granted. If a thing's character is more than its
    differences, a registering due to that character is a taking-up of its being that way. What the
    step needs is narrower: a taking-up of its being that way **in particular, as against another**.
    **Gap**: the reason proves less than the step.
  - What actually yields the step: the starting assumption read as "a relation to the thing in its
    particularity", together with (b) of P4. On that reading the step follows. On the other reading
    ("the relatum is some particular thing") it does not. The paper never says which reading it
    means. **Gap (γ on "in particular").**
  - **This step is the paper's central commitment.** It is the content of the author's first
    commitment in the draft (differentiation). It appears here as a patch between two principles,
    while (a) of P4, which is nearly trivial, is headlined. The architecture misstates where the
    weight lies.

**P12** (l.257–259). "Granted the step, what is taken up is a difference that holds, which is a
difference between terms that are both actual; that the terms of a difference that holds are actual
is part of what it is for a field to bear a contrast (Section 3)."
- "A difference that holds": if a is one way and nothing actual is the other way, it still holds that
  a is not that other way. So "holds" does not by itself give two actual terms.
- The second half supplies the missing step from a stipulated condition on the primitive (l.327).
  Then "actual terms" is true by how "contrast" was bound, not by this argument. **Gap (α)**, and it
  makes l.267–268 ("a consequence, not a separate choice") false as stated.

**P13** (l.259–262). P1 restated; not a reduction; "one reason why the dependence is stated as at
least partial". A second reason for "at least partly", beside the one in §5 (l.544). Consistent.

**P14** (l.262–265). "The registering, in turn, is some way in particular, and it holds; so it differs
from another **actual** condition, and the contrast between the two holds at least partly in virtue of
the first difference, since what one of its positions is, is due to it."
- "Is some way in particular": asserted.
- "So it differs from another actual condition": from determinacy one gets "there is a way it is
  not", not "another actual condition". **Gap.** Nothing in the paper shows that a receiver must have
  two actual conditions. This is the author's question of 2026-10-24 ("Why does R have to have two
  actual conditions and not just one?"), and it is still unanswered.
- "The registering" is first a condition (it "differs from another actual condition") and then the
  contrast between two conditions (P2 of the premise). **Conflict (γ).**
- "Since what one of its positions is, is due to it": a principle (a contrast holds partly in virtue
  of whatever one of its positions is due to) used once and stated nowhere. **Status.**

**P15** (l.267–270). "That every contrast in the account is between actual terms is thus a consequence,
not a separate choice… none of them is prior to registration."
- Not a consequence, by P12 and P14. For what is taken up there is an argument that can be made to
  work (P16). For the registering there is none: it is the author's decision of 2026-10-05 (Reading A)
  applied to the receiver. **Status (α, δ).**

**P16** (l.272–275). First place: in what is taken up. "An alternative cannot be that in virtue of
which anything holds. Where a term differs from nothing actual in a respect, there is in that respect
no difference that holds, and so none to be received."
- The second sentence is false as worded (see P12). The argument that works is different: where one
  side of a difference is not actual, only the actual side can contribute to anything's holding; what
  the registering is due to is then the thing's being as it is, not its differing; and by the step of
  P11 that is not a taking-up in particular. **Gap**, repairable; it needs the step and says so
  nowhere.

**P17** (l.277–282). Second place: in the dependence.
- "Neither of two contrasts may hold without the other": "may" is ambiguous; "it can be that neither
  holds without the other" is meant. **Wording.**
- The two cases show that the simple counterfactual is neither necessary nor sufficient for
  dependence. They do not reach refined analyses that also use alternatives. "It is not what
  dependence consists in" is stronger than what is shown. **Gap** in scope; the modest claim holds.

**P18** (l.284–290). Third place: in the receiver.
- "A repertoire therefore involves a relation that is not basic, **and it cannot be a precondition of
  the basic one**." From "X involves a relation that is not the basic one" it does not follow that X
  is no precondition of the basic one. What is shown: registration as defined uses no repertoire, and
  a repertoire brings in a relation of another kind. **Gap.**

**P19** (l.292–298). "Three things, accordingly, are not basic and are not provided by the core."
- "Not provided": holds, by the definitions.
- "Not basic": in sense (a) of P2 (prior to concepts and descriptions) this is a substantive claim
  about perception with no argument; in the other sense (not part of the bare structure) it is
  trivial. **Conflict (γ).**
- "Each requires what is built on registration, a repertoire or a pairing." P18 has just said that a
  repertoire need not be acquired. "Built on registration" contradicts it. **Conflict (β).**
- The note on "actual" (l.297–298) is placed at the end of this paragraph; "actual" has been in use
  since l.207.

**P20** (l.300–310). Standing. "An analysis… Its necessity is that of an analysis."
- Fair for what the notion contains. Not fair for the decision about the receiver (P14, P15), which is
  no part of an analysis.
- The description of Stroud's objection is an attributed claim made from memory.

**P21** (l.312–317). Basic form only; "in particular" is not "one only". **Holds.**

**Summary of §2.** What the section needs, as distinct from what it says it has:
1. That a basic epistemic relation is to something in its particularity. Assumed.
2. That particularity lies in differing. Asserted.
3. Hence: what is taken up is taken up as differing. (The author's first commitment.)
4. Receptivity: the taking-up is due to what is taken up. Marks out the subject.
5. That what is taken up is an *actual* difference: an argument (P16), using 3 and 4.
6. That the registering is itself an actual difference: **assumed**, no argument.
7. That alternatives are not required: shown for the structure as defined; not shown to be
   impossible as preconditions.

Items 3, 6 and 4 are the draft's three commitments (differentiation of the target, a differentiated
receiver, receptive dependence), and item 6 with "actual" is the decision for Reading A. The paper's
claim to have reduced them to consequences does not survive this reading.

## Ar. §4 Articulation (l.366–452)

**Ar1** (l.366–373). Articulation, terms, respect, contrast. "Its contrast is the difference of a and b
in that respect." An articulation that is not realized has no such difference; "the contrast it
specifies, if there is one". **Wording.**

**Ar2** (l.373–374). "A position is a place in a contrast that a field bears."
- At l.752–753 the two contrasts of the second realization "have the same positions and different
  respects". Then a position is not a place *in a contrast*: it is the thing a, present in both. The
  definition and the use disagree. **Conflict (γ).**
- With the footnote at l.386–389 ("the refusal to ground a difference in terms identified before
  it") this is a remainder of the reduction corrected at P5. The correction was made in §2 and in the
  abstract and nowhere else. **Conflict (β).** My statement to the author that no other sentence used
  the reduction was wrong: I searched for its wording, not for what depends on it.
- "Position" is used in four senses: place in a contrast (l.373); role in the relation C (l.573–574,
  l.681–682, l.875–876); relatum of C (l.777–778); place in an ordering (l.790–791). **Conflict (γ).**

**Ar3** (l.374). "A specified term is what a description picks out, and it may pick out nothing."
What is picked out cannot itself pick out nothing. A specified term must be an element of the
description, which purports to pick out a position and may fail. **Defective definition.** It has
been in the text since the six-round exercise.

**Ar4** (l.374–379). Specified; realized; why two levels; identity of contrasts "by stipulation".
- Identity by "same positions and same respect", with positions defined by contrasts (Ar2), is
  circular unless positions are things. Another reason to settle Ar2.

**Ar5** (l.381–389). Both actual; two terms because two positions; "nor does the schema presuppose that
a and b exist prior to the contrast as independent objects". The last is the author's neutral
sentence and **holds**. The footnote goes further than the sentence (Ar2).

**Ar6** (l.391–397). Two misreadings. "δ expresses the differentiation itself." Since the repair that
made δ the respect and the contrast the difference, this sentence is wrong: the contrast is the
differentiation. **Conflict (β).**

**Ar7** (l.426–429). Specification selects; can fail. **Holds.** (For "the condition stated in
Section 3" see F7.)

**Ar8** (l.431–437). δ*: "F does not bear a contrast in δ*, **because part of what makes δ* hold is not
in F**."
- By this reason, no contrast that holds partly in virtue of something outside its field is borne by
  that field. But the registering contrast K′ holds partly in virtue of K, which may lie in another
  field. By the reason given, R would not bear K′, and (C) could never hold. **Conflict** with (C),
  and with l.535–537, which allows a contrast of F to be partly made by a contrast of R.
- The right reason is narrower: δ* holds partly in virtue of *the account's own specification*. The
  conclusion at l.884–885 words it that way.
- "Makes δ* hold": respects do not hold; contrasts do. **Wording.**

**Ar9** (l.439–446). (D) and the lone term. Both follow from the definitions and the binding conditions.
**Hold.** Both use "registered" before §7.

**Ar10** (l.448–452). "The first limit of the reconstruction." No second limit is ever named. A
remainder of the draft's sequence. **Wording.**

## C. §5 Receptive Dependence (l.457–585)

**C1** (l.457–460). Why a relation is needed; "(C)… adds no commitment". **Holds**, given P2 of the
premise.

**C2** (l.462–465). (C): "A contrast K′, borne by a field, holds at least partly in virtue of a contrast
K borne by F."
- Called a thesis, written as a statement that there is such a K′. But the paper does not assert that
  registration occurs (l.302–303). (C) is the form of a registration, and "(C) holds between them"
  (l.648) treats it as a relation. Its kind is not stated. **Status (δ).** The first cold reader
  could not tell either.

**C3** (l.467–469). An account states an instance by specifying. **Holds.** "Does not wait on it":
figurative.

**C4** (l.471–474). Why unanalysed: analyses "either state it over alternatives… or belong to one
domain". Accounts of ground are neither; the next sentences mention them. "On offer" is too sweeping.

**C5** (l.474–479). The five conditions.
1. "As it figures in (C), it relates contrasts that both hold." **Holds.**
2. "Nothing holds in virtue of itself." Stipulated (l.531–532). **Holds.**
3. "It has a direction." Explained at l.530–531 as: the converse does not follow. **Holds.**
4. "It may be partial." This is a permission, not a condition that binds anything.
5. "Whether it holds is not fixed by any description of what it relates." **False of the paper's own
   second realization**: given that a is scarlet and b navy, it is fixed that they differ in colour
   in virtue of differing in shade. See C10.
- And the remark on "due to" (P9). **Conflict (γ).**

**C6** (l.480–489). Not ground; not counterfactual. The two "features that the literature on ground has
made familiar" are an attributed claim at level `record`. **Holds** otherwise.

**C7** (l.491–500). Resemblance and correspondence supply no dependence; (C) requires no pairing.
- Repeats P10. The same point is made twice, with different wording. **Structure.**
- Uses r and r′, which are introduced at l.620.

**C8** (l.530–542). Direction; irreflexivity stipulated; mutual dependence left open; transitivity left
open.
- The two questions are not independent. If the dependence is transitive, then from K′ in virtue of K
  and K in virtue of K′ it follows that K holds in virtue of K, which the stipulation forbids. So
  transitivity excludes mutual dependence, and mutual dependence excludes transitivity. The paper
  leaves both open as if separately. "If it is, **and no two contrasts depend on each other**"
  (l.541): the second condition is redundant. **Gap** (a consequence not drawn).
- "A view on which a difference in F is partly made in being registered is… not excluded": cannot
  stand with Ar8.

**C9** (l.544–554). Partiality; Bateson; MacKay.
- "For the same reason, the content of (C) should not be concealed behind appeals to 'systematic' or
  'relevant' coupling." No one in the paper has appealed to these, and "the same reason" has no
  referent. The sentence answers a text that is not there (the critique quoted in the first draft).
  The author asked on 2026-10-02 for exactly such references to be removed. **Conflict (β).**

**C10** (l.556–560). "Whether (C) holds in an instance is fixed neither by descriptions of the two
contrasts nor by what would hold otherwise. Two instances alike in their contrasts… may therefore
differ in whether (C) holds."
- True of the causal case. False of the constitutive case, where two instances alike in their

## What does not, in order of weight

1. **E7 / P2 / P19.** "Basic form" in two senses. The paper's main claim is not determinate until
   one is chosen.
2. **P11.** The central commitment (what is taken up is taken up as differing) is presented as a
   patch, argued by a reason that does not reach it, and resting on an unstated reading of "in
   particular".
3. **P12, P14, P15, P16, R4.** "Actual terms only" is not a consequence. One half has a repairable
   argument; the other half (the registering is a contrast between two actual conditions) is an
   assumption, and is the author's Reading A.
4. **Ar8 against (C).** The reason given for excluding δ* would make (C) impossible.
5. **C5(5), C10 against E3.** A binding condition on "in virtue of" is false of the paper's own
   second case.
6. **Ar2.** "Position" defined one way and used another; the reduction corrected in §2 survives in §4.
7. **P18.** The repertoire argument concludes more than it shows.
8. **C8.** Transitivity and mutual dependence are linked, and the paper treats them as separate.
9. **§0, I2, I3.** The count of what the paper rests on; the scope claimed in the introduction.
10. Smaller: Ar3, Ar6, F3, F7, G4, G6, G7, O1, and the leftovers.

## What the reading shows about the whole

The paper is two texts laid one over the other. One is the author's reconstruction: field,
articulation, receiving side, receptive dependence, registration, order, each with what it does and
does not establish. The other is a justification placed in front of it on 2026-11-10 (§2), which tries
to derive the first from principles, uses the first's vocabulary before it is introduced, and was
rewritten five times in one day. Most of the conflicts are at the joints between the two.

The draft began from three commitments said to be independent: that the target admits
differentiation, that there is a differentiated receiving side, and that the one depends on the
other. Over three weeks these were turned into "consequences" by moving their content into
definitions and principles. The content did not go away. It is now in the "further step" (P11), in
the unargued second actual condition (P14), and in receptivity (P9). On this reading the draft's
count was nearer the truth than any count the paper has given since.
