# The ground of D: report

Six rounds of formulation and critique (Claude), with five outside critics (fresh agents, no project
history, one per round from round 1 to round 5). Round files: round-0.md to round-6.md in this folder.
Nothing here is adopted; the author decides. Provenance: [draft] the author's draft; [agreed] agreed
with the author; [paper] the paper, partly Claude's rewriting; [Claude] Claude's own.

## 1. What the question turned out to be

The open point (mec/F-groundD) was: should D's ground guard against differences supplied entirely by a
description, by the receiver, or both. The answer is that the two guards belong in different places,
and that following them through changes what D is.

- The receiver guard belongs at C, not at D. Your draft already put it there (§3: "the receiving side
  could possess differentiations unrelated to the target") [draft]. It is two guards in fact:
  - the receiver merely differing within itself is excluded by C's "partly in virtue of";
  - the registered contrast depending back on the receiving contrast is excluded by C-asym [agreed].
- The description guard is your draft's ground of D (§1: "a differentiation attributable to its
  target, rather than one supplied entirely by an unrelated description") [draft]. It concerns the
  account's relation to its targets. That is descriptive aboutness, outside the core's scope under
  restricted P [agreed], so it is a condition of method, as the draft's own phrase "a necessary
  condition for that route of explanation" says.
- In the paper, D's ground is an argument about the receiver (§2) [paper, Claude's rewriting], which
  moved the receiver worry into D. That is where the problem in F-groundD came from.

## 2. Surviving formulations

### S1: L3, D split into a theorem and a rule of method (recommended)

Exact wording:
- Levels:
  - a specified term is what a description picks out, and it may pick out nothing;
  - a position is a place in a contrast a field bears;
  - "field F bears a contrast in respect δ" is primitive;
  - an articulation ⟨a,δ,b⟩ of F is realized iff a and b pick out positions of a contrast F bears in
    δ, with the direction of δ, if any, stated in terms of the respect, not by the order of slots.
- D-core (theorem): every field in which a registered articulation is realized bears that
  articulation's contrast.
- D-scope (method): the reconstruction articulates only fields taken to bear the contrasts it
  specifies, and concerns only contrasts the account specifies. Specification chooses the respect and
  selects a contrast borne in it; that it does not create the contrast is a stipulation of the route.
- The route does not argue against the view that all differentiation is supplied; it states that
  view's cost: if it held, nothing would be registered in P's sense.
- Exclusions:
  - E1-desc (scope, from D-scope): a respect whose holding depends, even partly, on the specification
    or its notation (order, labels, grouping of terms, for example), where the bearer is not the
    target, including respects relating target positions to notation positions;
  - E1-self (from C): a receiver differing within itself, where its contrast does not hold even partly
    in virtue of the target's contrast, is not registration; a field may register one of its own
    contrasts only through a distinct contrast, which follows from C-asym (nothing holds in virtue of
    itself);
  - E1-recv (from C and C-asym): a registered contrast does not hold, even partly, in virtue of the
    receiving contrast that registers it.
- Heuristic, method only: relabelling the terms, or replacing ⟨a,δ,b⟩ by ⟨b,δ⁻¹,a⟩, must not change
  whether the articulation is realized.

Argument: D-core follows from Df-registration ("A specified articulation realized in F is registered by
R when C holds") and Df-realized ("F exhibits it: its terms are both actual and differ in δ"), with
"exhibits" read as "bears". D-scope restates your draft's ground as a condition of method, which is
what the draft calls it.

What it excludes: the three exclusions above, each with its own source. The paper's single exclusion
("the view that all differentiation is supplied by the receiver") and the register's label "pure
constructivism" [Claude] disappear.

Cost:
- D is no longer an admitted premise of the core. Your draft says "Differentiability is admitted". S1
  replaces this with a theorem plus a rule of method. This is the largest structural change.
- D is non-modal: your draft's "a modal commitment: it concerns what F admits" must be revised.
- It is stronger than the draft on partial supply: a respect that depends even partly on the
  description is excluded, where the draft speaks of differences "supplied entirely".
- "Bears a contrast" is primitive and carries the whole weight of the description guard.

Provenance: the guard is the draft's [draft]. The split into theorem and method, the two levels of
terms, the heuristic and the three exclusions came out of the rounds [Claude, shaped by the critics].

### S2: conservative, D kept as an admitted commitment

Exact wording: "Every field the reconstruction articulates bears at least one contrast between actual
terms of its own; D does not say which, nor of what form." It is admitted on the draft's ground, the
route of explanation, unchanged. Levels of terms, heuristic and exclusions as in S1.

Argument: it keeps the draft's structure (D admitted, first, before R and C) and its ground verbatim.
It differs from S1 only in not reducing D to a theorem plus method.

Cost:
- A core commitment rests on a reason of method (the last critic called this a status mix).
- For registered targets D is redundant with D-core.
- It is non-modal, like S1, so the draft's "modal commitment" sentence still has to be revised.

Provenance: the draft's ground and order [draft]; the non-modal content [Claude, after the critics].

### S3: modal D, as in the draft

Exact wording: "F admits differentiation, without a particular differentiation having been selected",
read as a capacity of F, which F has whether or not it bears any contrast, and which is not a stock of
possibilities [draft; Ps-modal].

Argument: it is the draft's own reading, if the draft's "it does not follow that a differentiation has
been realized" means that F need not bear any contrast.

Cost, given Reading A [agreed], put by the last critic as a dilemma: stated without a reservoir,
"F admits differentiation" means "some articulation of F, if specified, would be realized". A realized
articulation has two actual terms. So the modal D is either S1/S2's D plus the possibility of
specifying (then nothing modal remains on F's side), or it needs non-actual terms, which Reading A
forbids. S3 can be held only by revisiting Reading A or by adopting the capacities branch (mec/B-power)
in D, where three critics found that a capacity cannot be stated without what it would do.

## 3. Dropped, with reasons

- G2r, relata-level receiver guard ("not all registered differentiation is supplied by the receiver"):
  contradicts the description guard when a field registers a contrast in itself (round 1).
- G3, both guards as one ground: redundant once each is placed (round 1).
- G4, "not supplied by anything other than the target": confuses where a difference lies with where it
  comes from (round 1).
- G7, description as registration: under restricted P a description is not a registration (round 1).
- G8, scope only: merged into G1 (round 1).
- G9, description guard in D and receiver guard in C: D is existential and cannot exclude a particular
  registered difference belonging to the description (critic 1, round 2).
- G5/6 and H2, D derived from P: the derivation equivocated between a difference between X and
  something else, and a difference within F (critics 1–2); as a lemma for registered cases it
  survives in S1's D-core.
- H1′ and the "in virtue of specification" wordings: they apply C's primitive to the account's
  relation to its target, which the core does not cover; "not wholly" also conceded partial creation
  (critics 2–3).
- K1a, D modal over specifications: "admits" became idle (critic 3).
- K1b, D as capacity: kept as S3, with the dilemma.
- The universality test for "merely notational" (critic 2) and the invariance test as a definition
  (critic 3): misclassified respects, or circular; kept only as a heuristic.
- E1-desc as "contrasts that exist only through being specified": empty, since specification only
  selects (critic 4).

## 4. My recommendation [Claude]

S1. It answers the open point by placing each guard where its source is. It removes the school label.
It keeps the draft's guard in the draft's own sense, as a condition of the route. And it makes explicit
what the paper left implicit: D, as the paper states it, does two jobs that come apart, a theorem
about registered targets and a rule about what the account may articulate.

Where I am least sure:
- Whether your draft's "without a particular differentiation having been selected" and "it does not
  follow that a differentiation has been realized" meant S3's reading. Your draft's statement of D sets
  "admits" against "selected", which is the account's act, so I read it as compatible with S1. But I may
  be choosing the reading that suits the result.
- Whether a primitive "bears a contrast" is acceptable to you. It carries the whole weight of the
  description guard, and it is not further analysed.
- The stronger exclusion of partial supply. I think it follows from "what the target bears", but it
  goes beyond the draft's "entirely".

If you want the least change to the paper, S2 does almost as well and keeps D admitted.

## 5. Bet and result

The bet (round 0):
1. The description guard belongs to D's ground. Right in substance: it is the draft's ground. But it
   ended at the level of method, and D itself split.
2. The receiver guard is a consequence of C. Right, and refined: two guards, one from "partly in virtue
   of" and one from C-asym, plus the question of chains.
3. E1 splits. Right: into three, not two.

Not anticipated:
- the modal question became central;
- D splits into a theorem and a rule of method;
- the two levels of terms (specified terms and positions);
- the ambiguity in Field-aspect;
- the problems with P's wording.

## 6. Decisions for the author

1. S1, S2 or S3.
2. If S1 or S2: the draft's sentence "a modal commitment: it concerns what F admits" is revised to the
   non-modal content.
3. Field-aspect: is "subject to differentiation" modal, or "bears a contrast"? The sentence from the

4. Partial supply: exclude a respect that depends even partly on the description (S1), or only one

## 7. Found along the way, beyond this question

- Is "partly in virtue of" transitive? If yes, E1-recv also excludes indirect loops, and C generates an
  order among contrasts: a consequence of registration, consistent with E5 and with the process frame.
  If no, E1-recv excludes only direct dependence.
- C-asym's wording should read "not even partly in virtue of"; the derivations rely on it.
- "Matched" in C has no definition that is neither counterfactual (alternatives) nor fixed by the
  theorist (a knower). Related to mec/F-pairing.
- P's wording, raised by three critics independently, comes from Claude's rewrite (the draft has no P),
  approved by you in restricted form:
  - "distinguishable from what it is not" reads as a negation or an alternative; proposed: "contrasts
    with some actual other term";
  - "depend on X" relates to a term, where C relates contrasts; proposed: "hold partly in virtue of
    contrasts in which X is a term".
- "Co-present" (Reading A, Df-conditions) has no stated meaning that is not temporal or spatial.
- A lone term with no actual other term cannot be registered: a consequence of P and Reading A, to be
  stated.
- A correction to what Claude told you earlier: the label "pure constructivism" is in the register's E1,
  not in the paper. The paper speaks of "a rival view … distinctions are made, not found", with a
  footnote.
