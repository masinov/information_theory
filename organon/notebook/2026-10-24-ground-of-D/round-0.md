## Observation before starting
The draft guards against the description in §1 (d1, d2), the ground of D, and treats the receiver's
own differences in §3 (d6), where R and C are introduced. The paper moved the receiver worry into the
ground of D (p2) while keeping the notation clause as an explanation (p1). So the question may not be
"which guard" but "which guard belongs where".

## Bet (written before round 1)
I expect to conclude:
1. The description guard belongs to D's ground, as in the draft: it is a condition on what the
   account talks about, and nothing else in the core supplies it.
2. The receiver guard is not a ground at all: stated at the level of contrasts, it follows from C and
   C-asym, so it is a consequence, not a premise. Stated at the level of relata ("not supplied by the
   receiver"), it conflicts with Field-aspect's open case of a field registering a contrast in itself.
3. "Both" therefore resolves into: description guard in D, receiver guard as a consequence of C;
   and E1 splits into two exclusions with different sources.
Confidence: moderate on 1 and 3, lower on 2 (the derivation from C-asym may need more than C-asym
gives).
