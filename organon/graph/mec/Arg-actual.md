Three reasons for the decision, one for each place where alternatives might be brought in. In what is taken up: D and C do not by themselves exclude a difference from a way that nothing actual is, but such a way has to be supplied, by a stock of possibilities (an assumption about what there is that the account does not make), by what receives (the third place), or by the account's own specification (which D-scope excludes). In the dependence: one thing may hold in virtue of another although a third would have brought it about had the second not held, and neither of two things may hold without the other although both hold in virtue of a third; so what would have held otherwise is evidence of dependence and, in this simple form, not what it consists in. In what receives: registration as defined uses no repertoire, and a repertoire would bring in a relation of another kind, since to stand for what is not the case is not to hold in virtue of it.

## Relation to Dec-readingA
The decision is the author's and stands. Its content now follows from the two principles, and the author's reason ("B needs some sort of alternative repository") is the third argument. Under rule M4 the basis moves from "both" toward commitment: it no longer rests on parsimony.

## Corrected (2026-11-10, Test-audit-1)
The third argument no longer says that a repertoire is a product of registration; a repertoire need not have been acquired. It says: the conditions of a repertoire stand for what is not the case; standing-for is a relation to something, and not a receptive one, since what is not the case can make nothing hold; so a repertoire involves a relation that is not basic and cannot be a precondition of the basic one.

## Revision (2026-11-10, rebuild after the close reading)
By Dec-rebuild. Reasons for a decision, not a derivation. Narrowed in three places (close reading P16, P17, P18): the first reason no longer says that a difference from an alternative does not hold; the second concerns the simple counterfactual only; the third no longer concludes that a repertoire cannot be a precondition, only that it is not used and would be a further commitment.
Previous wording: "What something is due to must itself hold; so what is taken up is a difference that holds, between terms both actual, and the same goes for the registering. Alternatives might enter in three places, and none is prior to registration. In what is taken up: an alternative cannot be that in virtue of which anything holds. In the dependence: dependence and what would have held otherwise come apart in both directions (K′ may be due to K although something else would have brought K′ about had K not held; and neither of two contrasts may hold without the other although both are due to a third), so what would have held otherwise is evidence of dependence, not what it consists in. In the receiver: a repertoire has to be held by actual conditions that stand for what is not the case, and such a receiver already registers and retains."
