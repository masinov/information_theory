What something is due to must itself hold; so what is taken up is a difference that holds, between terms both actual, and the same goes for the registering. Alternatives might enter in three places, and none is prior to registration. In what is taken up: an alternative cannot be that in virtue of which anything holds. In the dependence: dependence and what would have held otherwise come apart in both directions (K′ may be due to K although something else would have brought K′ about had K not held; and neither of two contrasts may hold without the other although both are due to a third), so what would have held otherwise is evidence of dependence, not what it consists in. In the receiver: a repertoire has to be held by actual conditions that stand for what is not the case, and such a receiver already registers and retains.

## Relation to Dec-readingA
The decision is the author's and stands. Its content now follows from the two principles, and the author's reason ("B needs some sort of alternative repository") is the third argument. Under rule M4 the basis moves from "both" toward commitment: it no longer rests on parsimony.

## Corrected (2026-11-10, Test-audit-1)
The third argument no longer says that a repertoire is a product of registration; a repertoire need not have been acquired. It says: the conditions of a repertoire stand for what is not the case; standing-for is a relation to something, and not a receptive one, since what is not the case can make nothing hold; so a repertoire involves a relation that is not basic and cannot be a precondition of the basic one.
