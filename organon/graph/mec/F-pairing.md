Matching pairs each term of the target with its own condition of R: a with r, and b with r′. The argument for C needs less: one condition of R that holds partly because of the contrast between a and b, and something else in R for it to differ from. That other condition need not answer to b. Both pictures register the same thing, that a differs from b; the pairing adds structure that the argument for C neither uses nor justifies.

## Where the pairing matters
Only in Which-position: sorting the target's terms needs a condition that goes with a specifically. There the pairing would enter as an explicit further commitment.

## Origin
Raised by the author's question why R must have two conditions rather than one.
