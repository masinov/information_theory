Registration of which way F is can be had from contrasts between actual terms, within a limit. R registers which way a term a of F is when the condition of R that answers to a through C is one of several actual conditions of R, each of which answers, through C, to some actual term of F, in this articulation or in another realized one. Which way a is, as registered, is which of F's actual terms a goes with: those whose condition it shares. The other conditions of R are relevant to F not as possibilities but because each answers to an actual term of F somewhere.

## What this changes in the question
The paper's question is whether R can register that F is a rather than b. Here "rather than b" stops meaning what F could be instead, and comes to mean the actual terms a falls apart from: a is grouped with the other actual terms that share its condition of R, and apart from those that share the other condition. Selection among possibilities becomes sorting among actual terms. Whether sorting is enough to count as which way F is, is the judgement on which adoption depends; what it leaves out is exactly the part that needs alternatives.

## Answer to the open point of Lead-repertoire
Lead-repertoire left open whether R's vocabulary says which way F is or only which way R is. The unoccupied conditions of R are relevant to F only where they answer, through C, to actual terms of F. A condition that answers to nothing actual in F is part of R alone.

## Limit
Which way F is can be registered only relative to the variety F actually has. Registering a term as one of the ways F merely could be needs alternatives (Reading B) or capacities identified by what they would do (B-power, against E4); under Reading A it is out of reach. A term unlike every actual term can still be registered as differing from all of them, but not as one possibility among others.

## Requires
A condition of R paired with each term it answers to (F-pairing; not provided by the core if Field-aspect is adopted). More than one instance of registration, or an articulation with more than two terms (Cv-two allows it). That a condition of R be the same condition in different instances: more individuation than Ps-individ admits. And, where the instances are not co-present, an order in which earlier ones are retained: beyond the co-present case, Q3 depends on Q4.

## Relation to F-single
In each instance, whether C holds remains a fact of that instance (B-single). What the plurality of instances fixes is not whether C holds but which way the registered term is. The proposal needs plurality for the content of which-way registration without deciding between B-single and B-reg: it separates the two questions.

## Cases considered
- A uniform field: nothing is registered at all, since D requires terms that differ. Consistent.
- A determination unlike any actual one: registered as differing from all actual terms, not as one possible way among others. This is the limit.
- A condition of R that answers to no actual term of F: it has no relevance to F. To say that R "could" register something through it is to speak of a disposition, and registration is defined as actual, not dispositional (Df-registration). Accepted as a cost of Reading A.
- Error: R occupies a condition that answers elsewhere to b, while here F's term is a. Then C fails in this instance: correspondence without registration. Error belongs to a later layer.

## Precedent
Saussure held that the value of a sign is fixed by the other signs actually in the system: in language there are only differences, without positive terms (checked against secondary literature only). The proposal applies this to the conditions of R, with one difference: R's conditions get their relevance to F from actual dependence (C), which Saussure's system, arbitrary in its relation to things, does not need.

## If adopted
F-which is resolved by restriction: which-way registration is available relative to F's actual variety; registration against mere possibilities is left to later layers that admit alternatives as an explicit further commitment.
