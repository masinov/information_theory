In its basic form, prior to concepts and descriptions, an epistemic relation is always a relation to something in particular. What is perceived, or registered in any way, is a difference: X as it differs, in some respect, from an actual other term. And the registering must depend on that difference; otherwise what is perceived or registered would not be about it.

## Notes
Motivates D, R and C. The core claims necessity only: P yields necessary conditions, not sufficient ones. P does not cover aboutness by description, of the future, or of mathematical objects; whether these are built from basic aboutness is Q5.
