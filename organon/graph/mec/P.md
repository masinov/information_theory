In its basic form, prior to concepts and descriptions, an epistemic relation is always a relation to something in particular. For X to be perceived, or registered in any way, X must be distinguishable from what it is not, and that distinguishing must depend on X; otherwise what is perceived or registered would not be about X.

## Notes
Motivates D, R and C. The core claims necessity only: P yields necessary conditions, not sufficient ones. P does not cover aboutness by description, of the future, or of mathematical objects; whether these are built from basic aboutness is Q5.
