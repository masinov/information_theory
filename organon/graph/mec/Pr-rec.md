The relation under study is the one in which something is taken up, and what is taken up is that to which the taking-up is due: a registering holds, at least partly, in virtue of what it registers.

## Notes
It marks out which relation is analysed; it is not a hypothesis that cases might overturn. Its content is shown by what falls short of it. Likeness, correspondence and ordering hold between two contrasts by how each is; if they sufficed, nothing would be received by anything. Agreement by arrangement owes whatever aboutness it has to a dependence somewhere in the case; with no dependence anywhere it is coincidence, which is of nothing in particular. Point of refusal: holding that an epistemic relation, in its basic form, need not be receptive, which the account regards as a change of subject. The word and the idea are the author's from the first draft ("receptive dependence", "reception requires a connection not supplied by either participant alone").
