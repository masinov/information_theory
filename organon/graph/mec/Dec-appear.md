The paper says "appears" where it said "perceived". To appear is to show up; the word is not used in the sense of seeming, and it implies no subject to whom something appears. "Perceived" and "known" are kept for where a subject is concerned: nothing is perceived that does not appear, so the conditions are necessary for perception, and the question of what a subject adds is mec/Q6. "Registered" remains the name of the defined structure: every appearing contains a registration.

## Reasons
The author: "perceived" "implies a subject more than i would like it and maybe we should reserve that word for the moment we introduce such an object", and proposed "what appears", noting that this is how the phenomenon is usually described. Claude's assessment of the candidates: "imprinted" implies a form passed over and a resemblance, both of which the account denies; "captured" implies getting the thing right and keeping it, and the core has neither fidelity nor retention; "received" suggests something handed over and nearly contains the dependence in its meaning. "Appears" puts what is under consideration first, attributes no act to the receiving side, implies no transfer and no keeping, and leaves C with something to say.

## A correction made on the way
Claude first said that "appear" wants a complement ("appears in"). The author objected that the phenomenon is ordinarily described as "what appears", with none. The word does not need one. That appearing always has a where is commitment C, and it should be stated as a commitment, not built into the word.

## Left open
Whether every registration is an appearing. The paper claims only that every appearing contains a registration.
