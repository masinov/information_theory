P follows from two principles. By determinacy (Pr-det), what a relation is of is some way in particular, and so differs in some respect. By receptivity (Pr-rec), the registering is due to what it is of, and what something is due to must itself hold; so what is taken up is a difference between terms both actual (P1). It is of the difference and not of X apart from its differing, because by determinacy X's being some way in a respect is nothing apart from its position among the differences in that respect. The registering is itself some way in particular, and it holds; so it is a position in a contrast between actual terms, and since a position is nothing apart from its contrast, it is that contrast which is due to the first (P2).

## What does not depend on Dec-readingA
That registration requires a difference and a dependence on it. With alternatives admitted, the difference might be from an alternative and the dependence might be stated over alternatives; there would still be the two conditions. What depends on the decision is their form.

## Status
An analysis of basic aboutness, argued and not proved. P2 is close to what "of" means; the argument shows what is lost by denying it, not that the denial is contradictory.

The earlier argument for P2 rejected likeness for not singling out K, which dependence does not do either, and presupposed P2 (Test-cold-1, defects 1 and 2). It is withdrawn as an argument. Dec-readingA is no longer a premise of the argument: P1 leaves open whether the other term is actual.

## Revision (2026-11-10, final of the day)
Rewritten around the two principles. The earlier versions either argued P2 circularly or called it a postulate; neither was right. Receptivity is what the relation under study is, and the argument shows what falls short of it.
