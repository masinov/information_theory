P1 is argued up to two steps; P2 is not argued. P1: for a relation to be of something in particular, that thing must be some way in particular in some respect; to be some way in particular is for there to be a way it is not (first flagged step); that other way is either the way of something actual or a way nothing actual is, an alternative, and there is no third case; so what the relation is of differs from an actual term or from an alternative. The clause says more: that the relation is of the difference itself, X as it differs (second flagged step). P2 is a postulate in two parts: the registering is a difference, by the first step applied to it; and that difference holds in virtue of the first, which says the relation is receptive.

## What does not depend on Dec-readingA
That registration requires a difference and a dependence on it. With alternatives admitted, the difference might be from an alternative and the dependence might be stated over alternatives; there would still be the two conditions. What depends on the decision is their form.

## Status
An analysis of basic aboutness, argued and not proved. P2 is close to what "of" means; the argument shows what is lost by denying it, not that the denial is contradictory.

## Revision (2026-11-10, after the first cold reader)
The earlier argument for P2 rejected likeness for not singling out K, which dependence does not do either, and presupposed P2 (Test-cold-1, defects 1 and 2). It is withdrawn as an argument. Dec-readingA is no longer a premise of the argument: P1 leaves open whether the other term is actual.

## Revision (2026-11-10, after the second cold reader)
