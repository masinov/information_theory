P follows from two principles. By determinacy (Pr-det), what a relation is of is some way in particular, and so differs in some respect. By receptivity (Pr-rec), the registering is due to what it is of, and what something is due to must itself hold. For the registering to be a taking-up of the thing's being this way in particular, and not merely of something that happens to be some way, it must be due to what makes that way a particular one, which is its differing from another. So what is taken up is a difference between terms both actual (P1). The relation is of X as it differs; X need not be nothing but its differing, and the registering may be due to more of X than that difference, which is one reason the dependence is "at least partly". The registering is itself some way in particular, and it holds; so it differs from another actual condition, and the contrast between the two holds at least partly in virtue of the first difference, since what one of its positions is, is due to it (P2).

## What does not depend on Dec-readingA
That registration requires a difference and a dependence on it. With alternatives admitted, the difference might be from an alternative and the dependence might be stated over alternatives; there would still be the two conditions. What depends on the decision is their form.

## Status
An analysis of basic aboutness, argued and not proved. P2 is close to what "of" means; the argument shows what is lost by denying it, not that the denial is contradictory.

## Revision (2026-11-10, after the first cold reader)
The earlier argument for P2 rejected likeness for not singling out K, which dependence does not do either, and presupposed P2 (Test-cold-1, defects 1 and 2). It is withdrawn as an argument. Dec-readingA is no longer a premise of the argument: P1 leaves open whether the other term is actual.

## Revision (2026-11-10, final of the day)
Rewritten around the two principles. The earlier versions either argued P2 circularly or called it a postulate; neither was right. Receptivity is what the relation under study is, and the argument shows what falls short of it.

## Corrected (2026-11-10)
The two steps that used determinacy as an identity are replaced (see Pr-det). The step from "differs" to "is taken up as differing" now rests on what a taking-up of something *in particular* must be due to.
