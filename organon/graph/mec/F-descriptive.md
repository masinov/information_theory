P covers any claim about X and requires that the distinguishing of X depend on X. Some aboutness seems to lack that dependence. A claim about whoever is the tallest spy is about that person whether or not anything in the claim depends on them: aboutness by description, as in Russell's theory of descriptions. Claims about future events, and on many views about mathematical objects, are about things that cannot act on whoever makes them (Benacerraf's problem for mathematical knowledge). If such claims are about their objects, P overreaches, and with it the claim that D, R and C are necessary for knowledge in general.

## Options
Restrict: P concerns basic aboutness, prior to concepts, which is the receptive case the core studies (E6). Aboutness by description, of the future, and of mathematical objects would be built from registered contrasts; whether it can be is a question for the epistemic layer. Cost: the core's necessity holds for basic aboutness only, and the thesis that all knowledge rests on it becomes an open question.

Keep P general: argue that descriptive aboutness depends on its object after all, through the registered contrasts the description is built from, and that knowledge of the future and of mathematics does too. Cost: it takes sides in long disputes in the philosophy of language and of mathematics, inside the core.

## Recommendation and decision
Claude recommended restricting, with the thesis that all aboutness rests on basic aboutness stated as an open question (Q5). The author decided to restrict (Dec-restrictP).
