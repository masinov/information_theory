The dependence in C is bound by two conditions in place of strict asymmetry: nothing holds in virtue of itself, and the dependence has a direction (that K′ holds in virtue of K does not bring with it that K holds in virtue of K′). Whether two distinct contrasts can each hold partly in virtue of the other is left open, like transitivity. If it occurs, there are two instances of C and each contrast registers the other.

## Reasons
Content: the strict reading was grounded first in the definition of roles (Dec-asym), then in the route condition (Dec-asymscope). The first cold reader showed the second ground fails: a contrast made partly by its own registration is still borne by F, unlike a respect supplied by notation, so the route condition does not exclude it. And the first ground is not needed: roles are relative to an instance of C, and with mutual dependence there are simply two instances. Nothing else in Layer 1 uses the strict reading; R needs only that nothing holds in virtue of itself, which is kept, because without it every contrast would satisfy C with itself. Test-formal-dep had already shown that strict asymmetry does not exclude loops. Method: P does not require the strict reading, and a restriction that is neither required nor well grounded should not be carried (Mp-parsimony).

## What changes for the author's earlier choices
The author accepted "not even partly" on 2026-11-09 and its regrounding on 2026-11-10, both on Claude's recommendation; this reverses Claude's recommendation, not a view the author had brought. The non-modal reading (Dec-fieldactual) is untouched. The view on which a difference in F is partly made in being registered (Locus) is no longer excluded: it is described as registration in both directions.
