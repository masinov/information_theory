Registration no longer requires a second contrast: a contrast K is registered where a condition q of something holds, at least partly, in virtue of K. And the paper states its central identification as a proposal: registration is the elementary form of appearing.

## How it was reached
The author passed the lean version to an outside referee and pasted the report, then Claude's reply to it, then the referee's second report. Claude judged the first report's three central points right: the paper moved between a necessary condition, a definition and an identity; the three things "not provided" sat badly with "to appear is to be registered"; and the second receiving condition was an arbitrary comparator. Claude proposed "elementary form" and dropping the second contrast; the referee agreed with both and drew the consequences listed below.

## Decided
1. **Elementary form.** Every registration is an appearing. Whatever else appears is claimed to appear through registrations that are part of it, not merely alongside it. The second claim is marked in the paper as not shown.
2. **One condition.** The receiving contrast is removed, not left unspecified: whatever contrasts R bears besides are no part of the dependence, and a receiver with a single condition is no longer excluded.
3. **No automatic continuation.** A dependent condition is not a contrast, so it does not automatically appear in turn; it does so only where it differs from something actual and that contrast is depended on.
4. **The dependence is on the difference**, not merely on how one term is. The balance satisfies this: what the inclination depends on is the difference between the masses.
5. **The second worked case is removed.** It had not been shown that the coarse contrast holds in virtue of the fine one rather than both holding in virtue of what the terms are.
6. **Small corrections from the report**: agreement owed to a third does not establish that neither depends on the other; contrasts between two terms are the account's domain, not a reduction; an asymmetric relation may run in a circle and is less than an order; the comparison with Dretske no longer claims the separation of source, dependent item and dependence as distinctive.

## Where Claude did not follow the referee
- The referee's formulation has "a specified actual determination". The paper says "a condition": "specified" names the account's act, which is no part of a registration, and "condition" is the paper's existing word.
- The paper keeps "it is not defined, since any definition would already use it" for difference; the referee would drop the reason. It is a claim about definition, not about what differences are grounded in.

## Reason for the change of substance
The second condition contributed nothing to the dependence: any actual thing differing from the first completed the contrast. It had been required only because the decision for actual terms was applied to the receiving side as if the dependent item had to be a difference. Nothing in D or C requires that.
