Two contrasts might answer to one another exactly, with neither holding in virtue of the other, because both hold in virtue of a third. If that is a case of K′ being about K, P2 is false.

## Answer
P does not count it as K′ registering K. It counts it as K′ and K each registering the third contrast, where the dependence lies. The verdict follows from locating what K′ is of in what accounts for K′, and nothing in the agreement distinguishes K from any other contrast that agrees as well.

## Later (2026-11-10)
Since P2 is a postulate, this is not an objection answered by argument but a consequence accepted with the postulate: each of the two contrasts registers the third.

## Final (2026-11-10)
Answered by Pr-rec: whatever aboutness an arranged agreement has is owed to a dependence somewhere in the case.
