The argument for D and exclusion E1 claim more than the ground of D supports. In the author's original draft, differentiability is admitted because the reconstruction "concerns a differentiation attributable to its target, rather than one supplied entirely by an unrelated description" (epistemic_core.md, §1): a condition of the route of explanation, not a thesis. The paper's argument for D turns it into an argument ("if every differentiation were supplied by the receiver, … what was registered would not be about F"), and E1 labels what is excluded "pure constructivism", which attaches the core to a position about a school of thought that nothing in the draft takes.

## Open point
The draft guards against differences supplied entirely by a description; the paper guards against differences supplied by the receiver. These are different claims, and the paper currently makes only the second. Which to keep, or both, is the author's decision.

## Proposed resolution
Restate the ground of D as in the draft, supported by P: what is registered has differences attributable to the target, not supplied entirely by a description (or by the receiver, if the author keeps that too). Rephrase E1 without the label, as a conditional exclusion: if all differences were so supplied, there would be no registration in P's sense. Both pending the author's decision.

## Resolution (2026-10-25)
Both guards are kept, in different places (Dec-groundD). The description guard is the draft's ground, stated as a condition of method (D-scope, E1). The receiver guard follows from C and C-asym (E1-self, E1-recv). D becomes a consequence of the definitions; the label "pure constructivism" is dropped.
