The conditions on C checked in two cases with dependences of different kinds (§8 of the paper).

## Causal case
Two bodies a, b that differ in mass on the pans of a balance whose ends stand at different heights. K: a and b in respect of mass. K′: the two ends in respect of height. Both hold, all positions actual (C-actual). K′ holds at least partly in virtue of K, and partly in virtue of how the balance is built (C-partial). K does not hold even partly in virtue of K′ (C-asym). No transfer (E3), no repertoire (E4). K′ also holds partly in virtue of where the bodies were placed, and so registers that contrast too (Dec-indiscriminate). The case involves a temporal process; the schema does not require one (E5).

## Constitutive case
Any two terms a, b that differ, and the sets {a}, {b}. K: a and b. K′: the two sets. K′ holds in virtue of K and not conversely; the dependence is not causal, temporal or spatial. It is complete: K′ holds wholly in virtue of K, and is still a second contrast, since its positions are the sets and not their members.

## Results
- The schema has instances in more than one domain; the conditions can be checked in each without alternatives.
- In each case the dependence is identified by the means of its own domain. The core does not supply them (Dec-schema).
- The schema is permissive: it admits the relation of a unit set to its member. The core does not mark off the epistemic; it states a structure every basic epistemic relation contains and much else contains too.
- A contrast holding wholly in virtue of another is consistent with its being a second contrast (answers what F-partly left open, and one horn of F-ground).

## Not tested
A case from a linguistic domain; a case where the two contrasts have the same positions and differ only in respect.

## Revised (2026-11-10, after the first cold reader)
- The constitutive case is restated with a named respect: a and b differ in δ; a taken with c and b taken with c differ in what their members are in δ. The unit-set version named no respect and left it unclear whether bare distinctness was being used as one.
- "The conditions can be checked without appeal to alternatives" is withdrawn (F-evidence).
- If collections are admitted, every contrast is registered by another (F-ubiquity).
- In the causal case the two contrasts are taken as they hold together, with the balance at rest (F-tense).

## Revised (2026-11-10, after the second cold reader)
The constitutive case with collections is withdrawn: the collections were formed by the account, and the scope condition leaves out respects that depend on the grouping of terms. Replaced: a and b differ in a fine respect δ (exact shade: scarlet, navy) and so in a coarser respect Δ (colour: red, blue). K is their contrast in δ, K′ their contrast in Δ; two contrasts, since the respects differ. K′ holds in virtue of K and is due to nothing besides; the dependence is not causal, temporal or spatial; one field bears both contrasts. The claim that every contrast is registered is weakened to: wherever a difference in one respect is due to a difference in another there is a registration, so registration is very widespread.

## After the rebuild (2026-11-10)
The second case is given in letters: δ a fine respect and Δ a coarser one, such that terms which differ in δ in a certain way differ, for that reason, in Δ. Three differences from the causal case are stated: K′ holds in virtue of K alone; whether it does is fixed by description; one field bears both. In the causal case the paper now says that something does pass from the bodies to the beam.
