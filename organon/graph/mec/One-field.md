There is one field: whatever is under consideration. Target and receiver are two parts of it, and which part is which is a matter of position in C, as Df-roles already says. The receiver does not distinguish anything: distinguishing is done by the account, for the receiver exactly as for the target (Df-specified). What the receiver needs is only that something definite in it holds partly because of the target's contrast; to be definite, that something must be a term of some articulation of the receiving part, which it admits as any part of the field does (D).

## Changes it would make
- Field and target come apart. The paper uses F for both; the proposal needs "the field" (everything under consideration) and "the target" (the part whose contrast is registered).
- Commitment R loses its independent content. "R admits differentiation of its own" follows from D applied to the whole field. What remains, that some part of the field holds something partly because of the target's contrast, is already asserted by C. R becomes a consequence of D and C, or a definition of the receiving position.
- Conditions of R are terms of an articulation of the receiving part, specified by the account. One of them holds partly because of the target's contrast; the other is the rest of the receiving part as the account divides it. No pairing of conditions with the target's terms (F-pairing).
- The argument for R is rewritten without "distinguish" (F-subjectR): C relates the target's contrast to something holding in another part of the field; that something must be definite; so the receiving part must admit articulation.

## Possible further reduction
The argument for R begins: a contrast in the target and a contrast in a second relatum are not the same fact. What it needs may be two distinct contrasts, not two distinct things. Then one part of the field could register a contrast in another part of itself. Whether C-asym and the roles survive this unchanged is to be checked.

## What it leaves for Q3
Which-position needs what the core would no longer provide: a condition paired with each term, and the same condition recognised across registrations. Both become explicit further commitments of which-way registration.

## Origin
The author's questions: why R must have two conditions rather than one, and why R should distinguish anything at all, since its differentiable part is part of the field. Worked out with Claude.

## Why withdrawn (2026-10-24)
It misread the author's point and overreached. It asserted a unity (one field), a part-whole relation (target and receiver as parts of it), and in effect a single container given first. The author's point was only that whatever is subject to differentiation is, insofar as it is, a field; not the same field, not parts of one, not from the beginning. Replaced by Field-aspect.
