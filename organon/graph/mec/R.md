R designates what bears the dependent condition. It needs one condition, not two: whatever contrasts R bears besides are no part of the dependence. Its condition can itself appear only as D allows, where it differs from something actual and that contrast is depended on in turn.

## Does not establish
A knowing subject. Whether R and F are distinct fields, parts of one field, or the same field bearing two contrasts.

## Revision (2026-11-09)
By Dec-fieldaspect, R is a consequence of C, C-asym and Field-aspect, no longer an admitted commitment, and is not modal. It rests on C-asym, which the register lists in Layer 2 although the paper states the asymmetry in §5. Previous wording, as a commitment requiring P and D-scope: "There is a second relatum R, distinct from F, that admits differentiation of its own."

## Revision (2026-11-10, after the second cold reader)
Retitled: one field may bear both contrasts, so "second relatum" and "second field" misdescribe it.

## Revision (2026-11-10, rebuild after the close reading)
Previous wording: "In an instance of C, the contrast K′ that depends is distinct from the registered contrast K, since by C-asym nothing holds in virtue of itself. What bears K′ is, insofar as it does, a field (Field-aspect); R names it."

## Revision (2026-11-10, one dependent condition)
By Dec-condition. R is no longer required to be a field or to be in two conditions. This answers the author's question of 2026-10-24 ("Why does R have to have two actual conditions and not just one?"): it does not. Previous wording: "Where a contrast K′ holds in virtue of a contrast K, K′ is distinct from K. What bears K′ is, insofar as it does, a field; R designates it, and it may be the field that bears K."
