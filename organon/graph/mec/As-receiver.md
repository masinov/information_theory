The treatment of the receiving side. The taking-up is some way that what receives is, and the account assumes it is some way in particular. By the reasoning given for D its being that way lies in its differing from another way, and by Dec-readingA the account treats that other way as actual. So the taking-up is taken to be one term, r, of a contrast between two actual conditions of what receives; the other, r′, need not hold in virtue of anything in the target. A second assumption: a contrast holds at least partly in virtue of whatever one of its terms is what it is in virtue of.

## Standing
The least secure step of the account, and the paper says so. Nothing in D and C requires that what receives have two actual conditions. This is the author's question of 2026-10-24 ("Why does R have to have two actual conditions and not just one?"). The answer now given is: by the decision for actual terms, applied to the receiving side; a receiver with one condition only is not covered. Neither assumption is argued.

## In the lean version (2026-11-10)
The paper says it in §5: both conditions are actual by the decision; it is enough that one of them is as it is in virtue of the contrast of F; a receiver with a single condition is outside the account. (C) is now stated between two contrasts, so the second assumption of this entry is no longer used in the paper.

## Revision (2026-11-10, one dependent condition)
Withdrawn by Dec-condition: neither assumption is used any more. The receiving side is not treated as a contrast between two actual conditions.
