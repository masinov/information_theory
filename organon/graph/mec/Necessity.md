The necessity the core claims is conceptual and conditional: necessarily, whatever is registered, of whatever kind, satisfies D, R and C, because this follows from what basic aboutness is (P, as restricted by Dec-restrictP).

## What is not claimed
That registration occurs, necessarily or at all: a world in which nothing is about anything is consistent. Nor that F is differentiated, or that R exists, apart from registration: D and R hold of whatever is registered and whatever registers it.

## Why conceptual
The arguments for D, R and C (Arg-D, Arg-R, Arg-C) use P and nothing else about the relata: no premise about what kind of receiver registers, whether physical, temporal, or like us. Whatever the concept of aboutness in P applies to, the conditions apply to. This is what "of any kind" can mean.

## Conceptual and metaphysical
If P correctly says what being about something is, the conditional also holds in every possible case, since what follows from what a relation is holds wherever the relation holds. The three readings are therefore not rivals. The conditional is conceptual, and metaphysical too if P is correct. The conditions themselves are contingent: they hold where registration occurs. Relativity to a receiver (Stroud's reading) does not apply to the conditional, but it survives at P (Obj-stroud).

## Where registration occurs
Wherever anything is perceived or registered in any way. That anything is, is contingent, and evident to anyone who perceives. Before P was restricted, the denial that registration occurs was self-defeating, since any claim was taken to be about something in P's sense. After the restriction, a denial made by description need not itself be an instance; the categorical claim rests on the occurrence of perception, not on the impossibility of denying it.

## Adopted (2026-11-09)
By the author, with the revision of the paper, where it is stated in §1 and §8. After Dec-groundD and Dec-fieldaspect the derivation reads: D follows from the definitions alone; C is required by P (Arg-C); R follows from C. So P carries only C, and the conditional is: necessarily, whatever is registered in P's sense satisfies C, and with it D and R.
