Whatever is subject to differentiation is, insofar as it is, a field. Being differentiated is not a property of receivers: it belongs to the concept of field, which the core already has (D). So the clause "R admits differentiation of its own" can be stripped from R and reduced to F's concept: insofar as anything in R is differentiated, R is a field, for that reason and in that respect. What holds for the target in the author's original draft then holds for any field, the receiver included: the differentiation concerned is "attributable to its target, rather than one supplied entirely by an unrelated description" (epistemic_core.md, §1). Nothing follows about whether R and the target are the same field, parts of one, or given before one another.

## What it changes
- The core needs two notions: field (whatever is subject to differentiation, insofar as it is: an aspect, not a kind of thing) and receptive dependence (C), whose asymmetry gives the positions of target and receiver.
- Commitment R becomes a consequence. For C to hold, something definite must hold partly because of the target's contrast; whatever has something definite in it is, to that extent, a field. What R adds beyond C is only that the two contrasts are distinct.
- Df-field changes: "field" names the aspect of being subject to differentiation; the scope of an inquiry is a separate matter (which field is under consideration as target).
- Conditions of R are terms of an articulation of R, specified by the account as for any field; no pairing with the target's terms is implied (F-pairing).
- The argument for R loses "distinguish" (F-subjectR): distinguishing is the account's, for every field, though the differences it picks out are attributable to the field, not supplied entirely by the account (see the statement).

## Consequence
Df-roles says the same relatum can be target in one instance and receiver in another. On this proposal that follows instead of being asserted: whatever receives is a field, and whatever is a field can be a target.

## Reading (2026-10-25)
"Subject to differentiation" means "bears a contrast" (Df-bears), not "can be differentiated": Dec-fieldactual.

## Left open
Whether target and receiver are the same field, parts of a larger one, or neither; whether one field can register a contrast in itself, given two distinct contrasts (to be checked against C-asym).
