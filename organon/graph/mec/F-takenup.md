The author, reading the rebuilt paper: "What exactly does 'take up' mean?" The paper says only that the phrase is used for the subject of the inquiry before any theory of it and is not analysed, with the gloss "perceived, or received in any way, by anything".

## What it is used to mean
Perceived, in the widest sense: the relation whose preconditions the account is looking for. It names what is to be explained. It is no part of the explanation.

## What is wrong with it
- The phrase is Claude's, introduced on 2026-11-10 so that "registered" would not be used in two senses. It is not the author's word; the author's words are "perceived", "reception", "registration".
- It is figurative, and it suggests that the receiver does something, which the account denies (R distinguishes nothing).
- Its extension is fixed only by its clear case, perception. "In any way, by anything" is not delimited. Yet the paper relies on a verdict about it: that in the second case of §8 nothing is taken up.
- The two commitments partly fix its meaning ((C) "says which relation the account is about"). So "every taking-up contains a registration" is substantive only to the extent that the term has a meaning apart from them. What it has apart from them is the clear case, and whatever makes a registration epistemic, which the core does not say (Q6).

## Not yet decided
Whether to replace the phrase, and with what. Nothing was changed in the paper while the author was reading it.

## Corrected the same day
This entry overstated. The author had asked what the phrase means, which is a question and not an objection, and Claude answered by calling it "the weakest term in the new version". It is not. Every analysis has to name what it analyses with a word it does not analyse; that the word's extension is fixed by clear cases, and that the analysis then constrains it, is true of any such word and is no defect. What remains is small: the paper does not gloss the phrase well where it first appears, and the phrase is Claude's and slightly figurative. The weakest step of the account is the one the paper itself marks, in §6 (As-receiver). Status set to resolved (dissolve): as a friction between D, C and the definition of registration it was badly posed.

## In the paper (2026-11-10)
The first use now says what is meant: "Taken up means perceived, in the widest sense of the word. Its clear case is perception by the senses… The phrase is used in place of 'perceived' only so as not to limit the subject to the senses, or to creatures that have them." The paper counts three notions that it does not define (being taken up, a difference, holding in virtue of), not two, and lists the first in its table.

## Closed (2026-11-10)
The phrase is no longer used. The paper says "perceived", in the widest sense (Dec-lean).
