Given Reading A, the question whether differentiability is modal is the question where the differentiation is located: in the field, or in a subject who differentiates. What separates the two locations is the direction of "partly in virtue of", not modality. On the first, the receiving contrast K′ holds partly in virtue of the field's contrast K; on the second, K holds partly in virtue of K′. Both statements concern actual contrasts. A "can" appears only when a field is described apart from any registration, which the core does not do (D).

## Provenance
The diagnosis (first sentence) is the author's, 2026-10-25. The restatement by direction of dependence is Claude's, 2026-11-09, correcting the author's step from a subject-side locus to modality; the author agreed.

## Two cases of partial supply
Partial supply by the description concerns the account's notation (E1, D-scope); the draft's "entirely" is about this. Partial supply by the receiver concerns R (E1-recv); it is decided by the reading of C-asym (Dec-asym), not by E1. An earlier reply by Claude (2026-10-25, not recorded) ran the two together.

## What remains expressible
An interaction that produces differences in F: K′ holds partly in virtue of K, and a further contrast K″ of F holds partly in virtue of K′. Excluded by Dec-asym: that the registered contrast K itself holds partly in virtue of K′.

## After Dec-direction (2026-11-10)
The core requires the first direction (K′ holds partly in virtue of K) and no longer excludes the second. Where both hold there is registration in both directions. The diagnosis stands: the two locations differ by direction of dependence, not by modality.
