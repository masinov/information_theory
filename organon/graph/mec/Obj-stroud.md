The core argues from P to its necessary conditions, which is the form of a transcendental argument. Stroud argued that such arguments establish at most what must be believed, or how things must seem, not how things are. Applied here: D, R and C might be necessary for registration *by receivers like us*, not "of any kind".

## Bears on
meta/Q-necessity. The author's question about neural networks (world, or perceptual hardware?) has this form.
