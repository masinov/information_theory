The core argues from P to its necessary conditions, which is the form of a transcendental argument. Stroud argued that such arguments establish at most what must be believed, or how things must seem, not how things are. Applied here: D, R and C might be necessary for registration *by receivers like us*, not "of any kind".

## Bears on
meta/Q-necessity. The author's question about neural networks (world, or perceptual hardware?) has this form.

## Partial answer (2026-10-24)
Stroud's objection targets arguments that move from how things must seem to how they are. The core's conditional makes no such move: it says nothing about how anything is unless registration occurs, and it uses no premise about the kind of receiver (Necessity). The objection survives at one point, P itself. Our evidence for P is our own case of being about things, so whether P says what aboutness is, or what it is for receivers like us, cannot be settled by an argument that starts from P. The available test is whether P survives in other frames (rule M5); in the process frame it survived unchanged (mec.process/Test-robust). The objection stays open, narrowed to P.

## Answer (2026-11-10)
Partly conceded. P is an analysis, defended by Arg-P and not proved, and the evidence for any such analysis includes our own case. What the argument does not use is any premise about the kind of receiver, so the restriction "for receivers like us" has no clause of the argument to attach to. What would count against P: a case we have independent reason to call a basic epistemic relation to K, in which nothing holds even partly in virtue of K. Stated in §2 of the paper (Dec-Pstatus).
