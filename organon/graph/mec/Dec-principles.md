The paper rests on two principles, determinacy and receptivity, from which P follows. That contrasts are between actual terms is a consequence, supported by three arguments about where alternatives might enter. The necessity claimed is that of an analysis: the conditions hold of every receptive relation to something in particular. The account says at which two points it can be refused. That the schema is met wherever one difference is due to another is stated as its main result, not as a defect: the base of the epistemic is not itself epistemic.

## Reasons
Content. (1) Calling P2 a postulate that no case can refute was wrong in the other direction from arguing it circularly: receptivity is what the relation under study is, and it has content, shown by what falls short of it. (2) "Actual terms only" was recorded as a decision resting partly on parsimony; it follows from receptivity, since only what holds can be that in virtue of which something holds, and each of the three places where alternatives might enter fails for a stated reason. (3) The same distinction between what dependence consists in and what is evidence of it resolves F-evidence and gives X-cf a ground that is not parsimony. (4) The permissiveness of the schema says something true: there is no primitive epistemic relation.

## How this was reached
After two cold readers the paper had been weakened at every point they pressed, and it described its own premise as unrefutable by cases. The author asked whether this was a collapse into scepticism; Claude then proposed strengthening it; the author objected that Claude was moving with each push and asked for a position held on its own grounds. This entry is that position. It was reached by working the question again from the start, not by adjusting the previous wording.

Taken by Claude under the author's delegation of 2026-11-10; open to the author's critique.

## Corrected (2026-11-10, Test-audit-1)
The paper rests on a starting assumption (a basic epistemic relation is to something in particular), two principles, and one further step (to take something up in particular is to take it up as it differs). Four points of refusal, not two. "There is no primitive epistemic relation" is withdrawn in favour of: the core contains no relation that is epistemic by itself.

## Revised (2026-11-10)
By Dec-rebuild, after the close reading: P does not follow from two principles, and actual terms only is not a consequence.
