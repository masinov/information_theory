The author, reading the abstract, asked whether "to be some way in particular is to differ" was meant as a reduction. It had been written as one and used as one in two steps (corrected in Pr-det). The author then asked: "Such a slip in the very first few lines and you already changed everything involved?" Claude had claimed a full read-through of the paper earlier the same day and had not caught it. This audit went through the paper section by section against the principles as corrected, listing findings before changing anything.

## Findings
1. Abstract: "alternatives… are argued to be products of registration" claimed for all three arguments what only the third said. Corrected.
2. The derivation attributed to determinacy that what a relation is of is some way in particular; that comes from the starting assumption (a relation to something in particular). Corrected.
3. The starting assumption had dropped out of the table when §2 was rewritten around two principles. Restored.
4. Substantive. The step from "what is taken up differs" to "it is taken up as it differs" was presented as derived. It does not follow from the two principles. It is now stated as a further assumption with its reason and its point of refusal.
5. That the terms of a difference that holds are actual is part of what "bears a contrast" is bound to mean (Df-bears); the derivation now says so instead of presenting it as a fresh result.
6. Substantive. "A repertoire is a product of registration" does not follow: a repertoire need not have been acquired. The argument that holds is that the conditions of a repertoire stand for what is not the case, that standing-for is a relation to something and not a receptive one, and so a repertoire involves a relation that is not basic. Corrected in §2, §7, §10 and the abstract.
7. "There is no primitive epistemic relation" claimed more than the realizations show. Corrected to: the core contains no relation that is epistemic by itself.

## What this shows about the day's work
The paper's source was rewritten by script about ten times on 2026-11-10. Errors were introduced by the rewrites faster than read-throughs caught them: findings 2, 3, 4 and 6 all came from the rewrite around two principles. A read-through by the one who has just written the text is weak evidence. No further restructuring should be made without a stated reason, and the next check should be the author's reading or a reader with no part in the writing.
