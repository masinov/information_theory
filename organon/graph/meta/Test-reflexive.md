The commitments, definitions and exclusions of the minimal epistemic core applied to organon. F is the investigation, the distinctions we draw in thinking about it; R is organon, its files, fields and links.

## Is the test well posed?
The objection: organon only stores what we write, as ink on paper does; the receivers are we. The core answers it through E6: a knowing subject is not a precondition of registration, so a receiver need not know anything, and ink on paper is not excluded. That our writing mediates between F and R is allowed, since nothing in C requires the dependence to be direct. The test is well posed under the core's own commitments. Adding a requirement that every receiver be a subject would make it badly posed, but that is a commitment the core refuses.

## D, R, C
- D: the investigation is differentiated (for example, one branch open, another closed). Passes.
- R: organon has differences of its own (two files, two status values). Passes.
- C: the difference between the files holds partly because of the difference in thinking (we closed X-cf because we found the contradiction) and partly because of organon's own organization (its vocabulary of statuses). C-partial allows this. Passes.
- Dependence also runs the other way, in other respects: the checker's warnings made us take stances and found the Nolan error, which changed the paper. This is allowed by C-asym, which holds between contrasts, not relata, and it illustrates Df-roles: organon is receiver in some instances and target in others.

## Exclusions
- E1, E2, E3, E6: pass. E6 passes because a subject in this particular channel is not thereby a precondition of registration in general.
- E5: registering the current state needs no order; retaining it (journal, git history, `since`) does. This is the core's split between registration and retention (Q4), found in practice.
- E4: see FR-repertoire. organon registers status by selecting from fixed lists, which are repertoires of alternatives; but the lists are actual parts of organon, not possibilities held anywhere.

## Definitions
- Df-specified: the schema specifies which articulations organon registers, and specification can fail. It failed once: two frictions fitted no kind, and the kinds were changed (ambiguity, overreach) instead of forcing the cases.
- C-local, found in practice: the bibliography entry for Nolan (1997) had the same form as a checked one, but its subtitle came from memory, not from the source. No inspection of a record shows whether it registers its source; that is why organon records verification levels separately. The levels are organon's practical answer to C-local.
- Df-articulation and Ps-relata: links are stored only on the dependent entry (38 requires links, none on their targets). The dependent entry is partly constituted by its links, and the target is unaffected by them: the asymmetric internal relations of the process frame (mec.process/Relations), not entries first and links afterwards. Only the file, as a record, exists before its links.
