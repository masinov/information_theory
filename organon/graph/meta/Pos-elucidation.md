Definitions have to stop in the bare intuitions that make everything clear. The leap from facts to language cannot be closed by definition; it is bridged by signalling, sketching and description. This does not discredit a theory, because there is no other way to operate, and the same holds at the foundations of mathematics, where difference, set and relation are appealed to as pre-mathematical terms. The goal of the investigations is to find the abstractions that are necessary for any other abstraction.

## Source
The author, 2026-11-10, in reply to Claude's "definitions have to stop somewhere": "Definitions have to stop in the bare intuitions that make everything clear. This is not a work of (deep) mathematics where we have to avoid circular dependencies… You have to appeal to intuitions of difference, set, relation etc as PRE MATHEMATICAL terms in order to introduce the actual mathematical foundations. There is no problem, because we cannot operate in any other way. There is an unsalvageable leap from facts and language that we have to bridge through signaling, sketching, description… Our goal is precisely to find the abstractions that are necessary for any other abstraction."

## What it bears on
- method/M6 (Claude's rule of the same day) treats a primitive as legitimate when it cannot be defined without circle and is bound by stated conditions. On this position that is not enough and not the main thing: a primitive is legitimate when the intuition is clear, when it has been made clear by elucidation, and when no other abstraction can be had without it. M6 is not revised here; it is to be reconsidered.
- The paper lists "two notions bound by stated conditions but not analysed" first among what it leaves undone. On this position that is not something left undone.
- A test the investigation has not yet applied to its primitives: can any abstraction be had without this one?

## Claude's two reservations, stated to the author the same day
1. An intuition can be clear and still be double: "in particular" and "basic" each turned out to have two readings. Elucidation has to be good enough to separate them, and stated conditions help to show when a word has been used against its intuition. They discipline the intuition; they do not replace it.
2. A circle among basic notions is harmless. Calling something a derivation when it presupposes its conclusion is still a fault, because it misdescribes what has been done.
