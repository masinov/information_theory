Written before the test was run.

1. **D and R pass.** Entries are distinguished (ids, types), and organon is a relatum distinct from the subject matter of an investigation, with differentiation of its own.
2. **C passes, partially.** The contrast between two entries holds partly because of a contrast in what they record, and partly because of how we chose to record it, which C-partial allows.
3. **E4 passes.** Branches are actual documents, not possibilities held somewhere; organon follows Reading A in practice.
4. **A tension with Df-articulation and Ps-relata.** Organon treats entries as objects that exist first and are linked afterwards, while the core says terms are positions within an articulation. Expected resolution: by distinction (an entry is a record of a term, not the term).
5. **An ambiguity in `requires`, against Cv-arrow.** The link is used both for "rests on" (an asymmetric dependence, like C) and for "was built after" (an order of construction). Cv-arrow says the same symbol must not denote both dependence and order. Expected: the sharpest finding, and a change to the schema.

## Outcome
1 and 2 right. 3 half wrong: branches are actual documents, but organon's vocabularies are repertoires of alternatives (FR-repertoire). 4 mostly wrong: links are stored on the dependent entry only, which makes relations internal to the dependent side, as in the process frame; the tension is weak. 5 wrong as stated: no `requires` link expresses order, which is carried by separate fields; the real misuse is `requires` used for "is about" (FR-requires). The finding predicted to be sharpest was not there; the sharpest findings (FR-repertoire, FR-partial) were not predicted.
