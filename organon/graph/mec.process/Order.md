Order is not presupposed by prehension but produced by it: one occasion is earlier than another when the second can prehend it. Chains of prehension yield an order that is asymmetric, and transitive through intermediate occasions. The order that acquisition and retention need (Q4) is then the order of prehension itself; nothing beyond C is required.

## Notes
Agrees with E5 (order is not a precondition of registration) and goes further: order is a consequence of repeated registration. Whitehead derives time from this order; whether every such order is temporal is not needed here.
