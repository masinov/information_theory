A notion may be left primitive when four conditions hold: any definition of it would use it, or something no clearer, or would readmit what the theory excludes; its use is bound by stated conditions, so that a misuse can be shown; it has instances in more than one domain; and the theory says what work it does.

## Origin
Answer to meta/Q-primitive (2026-11-10), extending the candidate criterion recorded there. Applied to "bears a contrast" (mec/Df-bears) and to "in virtue of" (mec/C-prim).

## To be reconsidered (2026-11-10)
meta/Pos-elucidation, the author's position, puts the legitimacy of a primitive in the clarity of the intuition, reached by elucidation, and in its being necessary for any other abstraction. This rule's conditions (no non-circular definition; bound by stated conditions) are then a check, not the ground. The rule is left in force until it is rewritten.
