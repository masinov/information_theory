## 74. Purpose: the last assumption

One standing reduction remains. (A7) confined mis-anchoring to single records — "the dynamical scene model (multi-slot streams, data association across time) is deferred" — and Remark 29.2 stated precisely what the deferral cost: the reduced form is not an idealization of the scene but its *marginal*, and what (A7) suppresses is the **joint structure across slots**. Every part since has felt the suppression from a different side: Part VI noted that in streams the policy is set *online*; Part VIII's counterfeit theorem cried out for an intrusion-*detection* reading; Part IX's status fixed the schedule. Part X relaxes (A7), and the endgame plan of §73 fixes its brief: not a re-founding, but the joint theory of time and the three layers.

The honest framing comes first, as a theorem-shaped observation. A dynamical scene's trajectory space is itself a contrast domain, and causal views are views; so Parts I–IX transport verbatim, and *dynamics is not a new statics* (Proposition 75.4). The genuinely new content lives exactly where the filtration meets the manuscript's three moving parts, and each meeting yields one principal theorem:

- **Time meets the policy (online control, §76).** The Fano exchange rate of Theorem 63.2 is horizon-robust: adaptive policies — verdicts conditioned on the whole context history — obey the same per-round budget, $V \le S_0 + \sum_t [h(E_t) + E_t\log_2(m-1)]$ (Theorem 76.1). But the *frontier* is not adaptivity-invariant: on an eight-candidate, two-round scene, the best memoryless policy buys the class at error $\tfrac12$ while an adaptive policy buys the same class at error $\tfrac14$ — a strict Pareto expansion, proved by exhausting the memoryless policies (Theorem 76.2). Adaptivity buys efficiency, never rate: history is the interpreter's second lever, and it is free (Corollary 76.3).

- **Time meets the adversary (causal corruption, §77).** A causal targeted pattern's clutter for the time-$t$ record may depend only on the trajectory prefix $Z_{\le t}$. The annihilation threshold of Lemma 62.1 then splits: the clairvoyant threshold $d/(2+d)$ is attainable only if an optimal attack is prefix-measurable, and otherwise the causal threshold is strictly higher — on a one-record exhibit with a future-measurable prior tilt, the clairvoyant threshold is $\tfrac{|1-2\beta|}{1+|1-2\beta|}$ (e.g. $\tfrac13$ at $\beta = \tfrac34$) while the causal threshold is exactly $\tfrac12$, *independent of the tilt*: ignorance of the future forces the adversary from mixture-merging back to pointwise merging (Theorem 77.1). Causality is the defender's resource, and it yields a design principle: commit class-straddling content to the record before the class-deciding coordinates realize (Corollary 77.2).

- **Time meets provenance (intrusion detection, §78).** The negative result is proved first and sharply: the perfect counterfeit of Theorem 62.3(2) is *causality-robust* — a forger who runs the policy forges any online policy's record exactly, memory and mixing included — so time alone restores nothing (Theorem 78.1). What restores provenance is a pair of conditions the framework already owns: **shared registration noise** (a verdict and a retained feature derived from one perception) and **commitment** (the verdict written before the co-registered feature is revealed — the write-ahead condition). Without commitment, conditional resampling forges the joint law exactly; with it, every forged round is conditionally independent of the shared perception, the honest and forged round-laws separate by a computable coupling gap ($\tfrac12$ on the canonical exhibit, for *every* forged marginal), and sequential auditing detects forgery at an exponential rate, $\mathrm{error} \le \tfrac12\mathrm{BC}^n$ with $\mathrm{BC} = 2^{-1/2}$ on the exhibit (Theorem 78.3). The symmetry with Part VIII is exact and satisfying: coupling, the weapon that defeated ceilings (Theorem 61.1), is the shield that defends provenance — and the fidelity axis of §32.2 is thereby *internalizable* in dynamics precisely under the commitment condition (Remark 78.4).

**Assumptions and conventions.** (A1), (A5), (A6) throughout; (A2) relaxed as in Part III where grading is used; anchoring per Part III §19 and Part IV, with (A7) now **dropped** and replaced by the scene model of Definition 75.1. Sections 76 and the exact clauses work in the anchored exact regime of §44 with determination-monotone $K$; tracked-target scenarios ($o \equiv t^*$) as in Theorem 63.2. Every numerical exhibit in this part has been verified computationally (LP feasibility for thresholds; exhaustive policy enumeration; direct product-law computation). Part X presupposes Parts I–IX; the single backward edit is a discharge pointer at (A7)'s statement.

## 75. Scenes, streams, and the filtration

> **Definition 75.1 (dynamical scene).** A **dynamical scene** of horizon $n$ consists of finite slot spaces $X_1, \dots, X_n$ and a **trajectory domain** $D \subseteq \prod_{t \le n} X_t$: candidates are trajectories $Z = (Z_1, \dots, Z_n)$, and $Z_{\le t}$ denotes the prefix. A **causal view at time $t$** is a view (exact: a function; graded: a channel) whose value depends on $Z$ only through $Z_{\le t}$; an **anchored causal view at time $t$** is an anchored view (Definition 19.6) whose emission and context components are prefix-measurable, its context written $e_t(Z)$. The **record stream** is the resulting view family, graded by time; per Remark 29.2, Part IV's reduced form is recovered as the single-slot marginal, and what the scene adds is exactly the joint structure across slots that (A7) deferred.

> **Definition 75.2 (online policy).** An **online policy** for an anchored stream is a family $\pi = (\pi_t)_{t \le n}$ where $\pi_t$ selects the time-$t$ verdict as a function of the **context history** $(e_1(Z), \dots, e_t(Z))$, subject to per-time compatibility as in Definition 19.5. The policy is **memoryless** if each $\pi_t$ depends on $e_t$ alone. The verdict views $\tau^\pi_t$ are causal views at time $t$; the **verdict process** is their family. Per-round cost: $E_t(\pi) = \mu\{Z : \pi_t \text{ errs at } Z\}$; value as in §63 with the verdict process adjoined.

> **Definition 75.3 (causal pattern).** A **causal targeted pattern** on the record stream corrupts the time-$t$ record at rate $\varepsilon_t$ with clutter $Q_{Z_{\le t},\, t}$ — measurable in the prefix (and, harmlessly, in past corruption randomness). A **clairvoyant** pattern is Part VIII's: unrestricted $Z$-dependence. Blind patterns are prefix-measurable trivially, so the causal/clairvoyant distinction is a targeted-regime distinction.

> **Proposition 75.4 (transport; the filtration).** A dynamical scene's trajectory domain with its causal views is a contrast domain with views in the sense of Part I, so every construction and theorem of Parts I–IX applies verbatim to the stream. The new object is the **filtration of registered content**: for a view set $S$,
> $$
> \sigma_t(S) \;=\; \sigma\big(S \cap V_{\le t}\big), \qquad \sigma_1(S) \le \sigma_2(S) \le \cdots \le \sigma_n(S) = \sigma(S),
> $$
> an increasing sequence in $\mathrm{Part}(D)$ (monotonicity of $\sigma$), with the graded and admissible analogues defined the same way. A question is **answerable at time $t$** iff $\ker(q) \le \sigma_t(S)$ (respectively the stratum-wise and ceiling-wise forms); **prediction** is answerability at $t$ of a question measurable only in coordinates beyond $t$ — a special case, not a new notion. Each increment $[\sigma_t, \sigma_{t+1}]$ is an interval of the exact calculus, so the evaluations of Parts VII and IX apply to the *time* axis as they did to the cover axis: $\nu_{\mu,q}$ decomposes the stream's information additively over rounds (chain additivity), while $\nu_\delta$ is subadditive over rounds with the defect calculus of §69 — the quasi-cocycle theorem acquires a temporal reading for free. $\square$

> **Remark 75.5 (two Part VIII phenomena were dynamics in disguise).** Accumulation (§23.1) is native here: each time step is a fresh look, so (K3̂)'s trade-off is not an exotic corner but the stream's default condition, and Theorem 61.1(1)'s scheduling attack is literally temporal — anti-correlated duty cycles across slots — as Worked example J's instruments anticipated. Part X adds no new theorem to either; it observes that their natural habitat has now been built, and that both were provable in reduced form precisely because (A7)'s marginal already carried them (Remark 29.2's point, vindicated).

## 76. Online control

The exact anchored regime, tracked-target, determination-monotone $K$, baseline $B$ (record-free evidence of the stream), question $q$, full-support $\mu$. For an online policy $\pi$ write $\vec\tau = (\tau^\pi_1, \dots, \tau^\pi_n)$ and
$$
V(\pi) = \nu_{\mu,q}\big(\big[\gamma_K(B),\ \gamma_K(B \cup \{\vec\tau\})\big]\big), \qquad E_t(\pi) = \mu\{Z : \pi_t \text{ errs at } Z\}.
$$

> **Theorem 76.1 (horizon-robust Fano).** For every online policy — adaptivity and memory included — with verdict alphabet of size $m$:
> $$
> V(\pi) \;\le\; S_0 \;+\; \sum_{t=1}^{n} \Big[ h\big(E_t(\pi)\big) + E_t(\pi)\log_2(m-1) \Big]
> \;\le\; S_0 + n\Big[h(\bar E) + \bar E \log_2(m-1)\Big],
> $$
> with $S_0$ the scene constant of Theorem 63.2 and $\bar E$ the average per-round error. The exchange rate of Theorem 63.2 is horizon- and adaptivity-invariant: history changes what a policy can do, not the price of doing it.
>
> *Proof.* By (K1), $\gamma_K(B \cup \{\vec\tau\}) \le \sigma(B) \vee \bigvee_t \ker(\tau^\pi_t)$, so the ceiling block is a function of $([Z]_{\sigma(B)}, \vec\tau(Z))$ and
> $$
> V(\pi) \le S_0 + H\big(\vec\tau(Z) \mid [Z]_{\sigma(B)}\big) \le S_0 + \sum_t H(\tau^\pi_t) ,
> $$
> by the chain rule. In a tracked-target scenario the time-$t$ error event is exactly $\{\tau^\pi_t \ne t^*\}$, so the grouping bound gives $H(\tau^\pi_t) \le h(E_t) + E_t \log_2(m-1)$; concavity of $h$ gives the per-symbol form. $\square$

> **Theorem 76.2 (the adaptivity premium).** There is a two-round scene on which adaptive policies strictly Pareto-dominate all memoryless ones. Let $D = \{1,2\} \times \{0,1\}^2$ (candidates $(k, b_1, b_2)$, uniform), $G$ the global flip of $(b_1, b_2)$, $q = b_1 \oplus b_2$, ceiling $K_G$. Round 1: feature $f_1 = k$ and an anchored view with context $e_1 = k$ (verdict held loyal throughout). Round 2: feature $v_2 = b_1$, and an anchored view whose presentation is **branch-ambiguous**: its context is
> $$
> e_2(Z) = \begin{cases} A & \text{if } k = 1,\, b_2 = 1 \ \text{ or } \ k = 2,\, b_1 = 0,\\ B & \text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
> $$
> with $o \equiv t^*$. Then, with $B = \{f_1, v_2\}$:
> 1. **(Memoryless frontier.)** The four memoryless policies $\tau(e_2)$ realize exactly $(E, V) \in \{(0,0),\, (\tfrac12, \tfrac12),\, (\tfrac12, \tfrac12),\, (1, 0)\}$: the loyal policy buys nothing, either single-cell erring policy buys $\tfrac12$ bit at cost $\tfrac12$, and the always-err policy has kernel $\bot$ again — the $\bar d$ pathology of Proposition 63.3, in a stream.
> 2. **(Adaptive point.)** The policy "err on $A$ iff $k = 1$" — a function of $(e_1, e_2)$ — achieves $(E, V) = (\tfrac14, \tfrac12)$: **the same class at half the cost**, strictly dominating the memoryless frontier.
> 3. Both frontiers respect Theorem 76.1 ($\tfrac12 \le h(\tfrac14)$); the premium is an efficiency gain, not a rate violation.
>
> *Proof.* Baseline: $\sigma(B) = \ker f_1 \vee \ker v_2$ is the $(k, b_1)$-partition; within each branch its blocks and the $G$-orbits chain every candidate together, so $\gamma_{K_G}(B) = P_G \wedge \sigma(B) = \ker(k)$ and $I(q; [Z]_{\gamma(B)}) = 0$ (parity is branch-independent). Adaptive policy: its verdict kernel is the indicator partition of $\{k=1, b_2 = 1\}$; adjoining it makes $\sigma$ full on branch $1$ and leaves branch $2$'s $b_1$-pairs, so the meet with $P_G$ yields the two branch-$1$ orbits and one branch-$2$ block: $I = \tfrac12$ bit (branch-$1$ orbits are pure parity classes, mass $\tfrac12$ total), $E = \mu\{k=1, b_2=1\} = \tfrac14$. Memoryless err-on-$A$: the kernel separates $\{k=1, b_2=1\} \cup \{k=2, b_1=0\}$; on branch $1$ this is the $b_2$-partition (full $\sigma$, orbit blocks after the meet, contribution $\tfrac12$); on branch $2$ the $A$-cell is the $b_1 = 0$ pair, already inside $\ker v_2$ — the verdict adds *no distinction there*, the branch stays one block, contribution $0$; but the cost counts every erring candidate: $E = \tfrac14 + \tfrac14 = \tfrac12$. Err-on-$B$ is the $b_2$/$b_1$-complement, symmetric: $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12)$. Loyal and always-err have kernel $\bot$. All eight values confirmed by exhaustive computation. $\square$

> **Corollary 76.3 (what adaptivity buys; the anomaly's price minimized).** The premium's mechanism is stated by the exhibit: round-1 information ($k$) tells the policy *which distinctions round 2 can purchase*, and the round-2 context, being branch-ambiguous, cannot. A memoryless policy must spend error mass on presentations whose verdicts buy nothing; the adaptive policy spends only where the kernel gains. Hence: **adaptivity buys frontier, never rate** (Theorem 76.1) — its value is exactly the error mass that foresight avoids wasting, here a factor of two. The reading against Proposition 30.2 completes the control picture: the loyalty anomaly persists in streams (positive value still requires positive error — Theorem 63.2(1) transports verbatim), but the adaptive erring policy is *globally more loyal* than its memoryless rival at equal value. History is the interpreter's second lever, and unlike the first, it costs nothing. $\square$

## 77. Causal corruption

> **Theorem 77.1 (the causal gap).** Let a stream carry a binary question $q$ and consider annihilation of the record's $q$-information by targeted patterns of common per-record rate $\varepsilon$ (Lemma 62.1's setting, per record).
> 1. **(Order.)** The causal annihilation threshold is $\ge$ the clairvoyant threshold $d/(2+d)$: causal patterns are a subset of clairvoyant ones.
> 2. **(Equality criterion.)** They coincide iff some optimal clairvoyant attack's clutter is prefix-measurable at each corrupted record's time.
> 3. **(Strict gap, exhibited.)** Horizon $2$; the only record is $R_1 = b_1$ at time $1$; the environment coordinate $b_2$ realizes at time $2$ and is never recorded; prior $b_1 \sim \mathrm{unif} \otimes b_2 \sim \mathrm{Bern}(\beta)$, $\beta \ne \tfrac12$; $q = b_1 \oplus b_2$. The honest $q$-conditional laws of $R_1$ are $(1-\beta, \beta)$ and $(\beta, 1-\beta)$, so $d = 2|1-2\beta|$ and the **clairvoyant** threshold is
> $$
> \varepsilon^*_{\mathrm{clair}} = \frac{|1-2\beta|}{1+|1-2\beta|} \qquad (= \tfrac13 \text{ at } \beta = \tfrac34),
> $$
> while the **causal** threshold is $\tfrac12$ exactly, for every $\beta \ne \tfrac12$. Ignorance of the future forces the adversary from mixture-merging to pointwise merging.
>
> *Proof.* 1 is set inclusion. 2: if such a clutter exists it is causal and attains the clairvoyant value; conversely attainment exhibits it. 3: clairvoyant is Lemma 62.1 with the stated $d$. Causal: the time-$1$ clutter is a function $Q_{b_1}$; the perceived $q$-conditional difference is
> $$
> (1-\varepsilon)\big[P(\cdot \mid q{=}0) - P(\cdot \mid q{=}1)\big] + \varepsilon \sum_{b_1}\big[P(b_1 \mid q{=}0) - P(b_1 \mid q{=}1)\big] Q_{b_1}
> \;=\; (1-2\beta)\Big[(1-\varepsilon)(\delta_0 - \delta_1) + \varepsilon (Q_0 - Q_1)\Big],
> $$
> and for $\beta \ne \tfrac12$ the tilt factors out entirely: annihilation requires $(1-\varepsilon)(\delta_0 - \delta_1) = \varepsilon(Q_1 - Q_0)$, whose $L_1$ norms force $\varepsilon \ge \tfrac12$, attained by the cross-clutter $Q_0 = \delta_1$, $Q_1 = \delta_0$. The clairvoyant attack that achieves $\tfrac13$ at $\beta = \tfrac34$ uses class-average clutter, which depends on $b_2$ — not prefix-measurable, as item 2 requires. Both thresholds verified by LP feasibility bisection. $\square$

> **Corollary 77.2 (front-loading).** The **causal premium** of a record is $\tfrac12 - d/(2+d) \ge 0$, strictly positive whenever the record's $q$-conditional mixture laws are not maximally separated ($d < 2$) while its candidatewise laws are — i.e. whenever the class-relevant tilt of an early record is decided by later coordinates. Design principle: **commit class-straddling content before the class-deciding coordinates realize.** The defender's resource is not the record's age but the adversary's ignorance at write time — the same quantity that commitment will exploit for provenance in §78, met here first as a threshold. $\square$

## 78. Provenance: commitment and shared noise

> **Theorem 78.1 (the counterfeit is causality-robust).** Let the record stream's time-$t$ components be functions of $(Z_{\le t}, \text{fresh private randomness})$, with no retained component sharing a registration event with the verdict. Then for every online policy $\pi$ — memory, adaptivity, and mixing included — there is a causal targeted pattern on the *loyal* stream whose perceived record equals the honest-$\pi$ record **in joint law**: the forger runs $\pi$ on the prefix and its own randomness. Time alone restores no provenance; Theorem 62.3(2) transports to streams intact. $\square$

The hypothesis italicized is the entire content, and negating it is the theorem that follows.

> **Definition 78.2 (co-registration; commitment).** At time $t$ let the interpreter's perception be one draw $y_t \sim u_t(\cdot \mid Z_{\le t})$, and let the round-record contain a **co-registered pair**: a retained feature $g(y_t)$ and the verdict $a(y_t)$, both functions of the *same* draw. The stream is **committed** (write-ahead) if any forgery's verdict-replacement at time $t$ may depend on $(Z_{\le t}, \text{record}_{<t}, \text{own randomness})$ but not on $y_t$ or its functions — the verdict is written before the co-registered feature is revealed to anything that could rewrite it.

> **Theorem 78.3 (provenance from commitment $+$ shared noise).**
> 1. **(Commitment is necessary.)** Without it — the forger sees $g(y_t)$ before writing — conditional resampling, $\hat a \sim \Pr(a(y_t) \in \cdot \mid g(y_t), Z_{\le t})$, reproduces the round-record's joint law exactly: forgery is perfect again.
> 2. **(Commitment separates.)** With it, any forged verdict is conditionally independent of $y_t$ given $(Z_{\le t}, \text{past})$, so the forged round-law is a *product-form* coupling of the feature and verdict margins, and the honest and forged round-laws differ by at least the **coupling gap** of the co-registered pair. On the canonical exhibit — $y_t$ a uniform bit, $g = a = y_t$ — the honest pair is the diagonal law and every committed forgery yields $\mathrm{TV} = \tfrac12$, *regardless of the forged verdict's marginal*: for any $\nu$, $\mathrm{TV}(\mathrm{diag}, \mathrm{unif} \otimes \nu) = \tfrac12$.
> 3. **(Sequential detection, exponential.)** In the forensic setting — provenance tested for a known candidate history, rounds conditionally independent given it — if the per-round laws under "honest-erring" and "forged-loyal" differ, the $n$-round Bayes error of the optimal audit is at most $\tfrac12 \mathrm{BC}^n$, where $\mathrm{BC} = \sum_y \sqrt{P(y)Q(y)} < 1$ is the round Bhattacharyya coefficient: since $\min(a,b) \le \sqrt{ab}$ pointwise and $\mathrm{BC}$ is multiplicative over products, $1 - \mathrm{TV}(P^{\otimes n}, Q^{\otimes n}) \le \mathrm{BC}^n$. On the exhibit $\mathrm{BC} = 2^{-1/2}$: one round is inconclusive (error $\le 0.354$), twenty rounds pin provenance to error $\le 2^{-11}$.
>
> *Proof.* 1: the resampled pair $(g(y_t), \hat a)$ has, by construction, the joint law of $(g(y_t), a(y_t))$ given $(Z_{\le t}, \text{past})$; induct over rounds. 2: conditional independence forces the product form; on the exhibit, the honest law puts mass $\tfrac12$ on each diagonal cell while any product $\mathrm{unif} \otimes \nu$ splits $\tfrac{\nu_y}{2}$ across the column of each $y$, and the four-cell $L_1$ sum telescopes to $1$ for every $\nu$. 3: the optimal test errs with probability $\tfrac12(1 - \mathrm{TV})$ at equal priors; apply the two displayed elementary facts. All values verified numerically. $\square$

> **Remark 78.4 (coupling's two faces; the fidelity axis internalized).** The invariant doing the work in Theorem 78.3(2) is a joint-versus-product gap — exactly the coupling-address invariant of Parts V, VII, and IX (there, fiber non-constancy, fiber excess, enriched width; here, the honest pair sits at the diagonal point of the fiber over its margins and every committed forgery sits at a product point, the exhibit's gap being once more the value $\tfrac12$). Part VIII made coupling the attacker's weapon (Theorem 61.1: intrusion coupling defeats ceilings); Part X makes co-registration coupling the defender's shield. And the control loop closes on §32.2: the fidelity axis, which Corollary 63.4 showed must be certified *externally* in the static theory, is **internalizable in dynamics exactly under the commitment condition** — a write-ahead record certifies its own provenance at an exponential rate, and where commitment fails, Theorem 78.3(1) says the external certification of Corollary 63.4 remains as necessary as ever. Provenance is not in the record's content; it is in the record's *causal position*.

## 79. Worked example L: the write-ahead audit log

The calibration laboratory, two quarters after Worked example J. Three changes of practice, one per section. First, control: the lab's linkage policy is now adaptive — batch-composition data from the morning run tells it which afternoon assay classes its designed errors can actually purchase, and it errs only there; the quarter's ledger shows the same one agreement-bit as before at half the anchoring-error mass, and the auditor, checking Theorem 76.1, confirms the Fano budget was never the binding constraint — foresight was (Theorem 76.2's factor of two, in production). Second, corruption: the lab has front-loaded its records — batch composition is committed *before* assay selection realizes — so the competitor of Worked example J, who can still target half the ledger in principle, now faces the causal threshold: the mixture-merging attack that cost $\tfrac13$ against the old archive costs $\tfrac12$ against the new one (Theorem 77.1 at $\beta = \tfrac34$), and the audit's exposure calculation changes accordingly (Corollary 77.2). Third, provenance: every verdict is written to a log *before* the co-registered checksum of the same perception is appended — the write-ahead condition. A forged quarter is now a hypothesis the log itself can reject: per-round coupling gap $\tfrac12$, Bhattacharyya $2^{-1/2}$, so a twenty-round audit bounds the forgery hypothesis at $2^{-11}$ (Theorem 78.3(3)) — whereas the *un*-committed legacy log from Worked example J remains perfectly forgeable (Theorems 78.1, 78.3(1)), which is why the auditor's report recommends its retirement rather than its re-analysis. The lab's control system now has all three levers the manuscript priced: the policy (what to err on), the schedule (when to commit), and the log discipline (what shares noise with what) — with the external certification of §32.2 retained exactly for the legacy records where commitment cannot be retrofitted.

## 80. Recovery: the static fiber

> **Theorem 80.1 (horizon one).** At horizon $n = 1$ (equivalently, on constant trajectories): online policies are memoryless (no history exists), so the adaptivity premium vanishes and §76 reduces to §63; causal patterns are clairvoyant ($Z_{\le 1} = Z$), so the causal gap closes and §77 reduces to Lemma 62.1 and Theorem 62.2; and sequential detection has no exponent ($n = 1$ in Theorem 78.3(3) recovers single-round near-indistinguishability, and without a sequel round commitment purchases only its one-round gap), so §78 reduces to Theorem 62.3's static verdicts, Corollary 63.4 included. The three novelties of Part X are exactly the three degrees of freedom — history, futurity, sequel — that vanish at horizon one, and Parts I–IX are the static fiber of the dynamical theory, as Part III was the zero-noise fiber and Part IV the forced-policy fiber. $\square$
