## 83. The multi-domain frame

> **Definition 83.1 (ambient class; carving; domain family).** Fix an ambient class $\Omega$ of targets — the universe from which contrast domains are carved (§2's deferred clause, now cashed). A **domain family** is a finite diagram $\mathcal{D}$ of contrast domains, each carrying its own view family, connected by generating morphisms of the three kinds H5 names:
> - **coarsening** $c_\pi : D \twoheadrightarrow D/\pi$ for $\pi \in \mathrm{Part}(D)$ — a question, promoted to a domain (the identification that makes Part VII's ledger functor, Definition 56.1, a *domain morphism*: prior-marginalization is coarsening along $\ker q$, with channels pushed forward by prior-averaging);
> - **restriction** $D' \hookrightarrow D$ for $D' \subseteq D$ — fewer live alternatives (conditioning; sub-cohorts); its reverse reading is **refinement**, traveling backward along a coarsening;
> - **joint domains** $D_1 \times D_2$ for distinct targets carved from $\Omega$ — the multi-target case, with factor-local view families (the constructor of Worked example I).
>
> Views transport contravariantly along coarsenings and restrictions (precomposition; trace) and factor-locally into products; graded families transport with them (pushforward of channels along $c_\pi$ with prior-averaging; restriction of kernels; factor tensors), coherence being preserved by marginality in each case.

> **Lemma 83.2 (transport calculus; the part's core).**
> 1. **(Coarsening: a lattice isomorphism onto an ideal.)** The pullback $c_\pi^* : \mathrm{Part}(D/\pi) \to \mathrm{Part}(D)$, $\bar\rho \mapsto \{c_\pi^{-1}(B) : B \in \bar\rho\}$, is an isomorphism of complete lattices onto the principal ideal ${\downarrow}\pi$ — the partitions coarser than $\pi$, i.e. the $\pi$-saturated ones. It preserves arbitrary joins (preimages of intersections are intersections of preimages) **and arbitrary meets**: a transitive-closure chain downstairs lifts, because within-fiber moves are available by reflexivity, so $c_\pi^*(\bar\rho \wedge \bar\rho') = c_\pi^*\bar\rho \wedge c_\pi^*\bar\rho'$. Consequently **every formula built from kernels and the two lattice operations — contents $\sigma$, ceilings $\gamma_K = P_G \wedge \sigma$, verdict adjunctions, filtrations — transports verbatim along coarsenings.**
> 2. **(Restriction: exact on joins, lax on meets.)** The trace $\rho \mapsto \rho|_{D'}$ commutes with joins (blockwise intersection with $D'$ commutes with common refinement) but only laxly with meets, with the definite direction
> $$
> (\rho \wedge \rho')\big|_{D'} \;\le\; \rho|_{D'} \wedge \rho'|_{D'},
> $$
> strictly in general: on $D = \{1,2,3\}$ with $\rho = \{12 \mid 3\}$, $\rho' = \{1 \mid 23\}$, and $D' = \{1,3\}$, the $D$-meet is the single block (the chain runs through the excluded candidate $2$) while the traced meet is discrete. The mechanism is the manuscript's familiar one — a closure operator (transitive closure) failing to commute with a second operation — now in its third guise after the tolerance reflection of Proposition 22.3′ and the orbit reflection of Theorem 37.7: **chains of identification may pass through candidates that a restriction removes.**
> 3. **(Products: both operations factorize.)** For factor-local data, $(\rho_1 \times \rho_2) \vee (\rho_1' \times \rho_2') = (\rho_1 \vee \rho_1') \times (\rho_2 \vee \rho_2')$ and likewise for $\wedge$: a transitive-closure chain in the product interleaves into per-factor chains, holding the other coordinate fixed by reflexivity, and conversely per-factor chains concatenate. Hence contents *and ceilings* over factor-local views factorize — the lemma used ad hoc in Worked example I, established once.
>
> *Proof.* All three as sketched, each direction elementary; 1 and 3 additionally machine-verified on 200 randomized instances each, and 2's counterexample computed. $\square$

> **Proposition 83.3 (functoriality of $\sigma$; Theorem 6.2 extended; H5's first obligation).** Along the three constructors:
> $$
> \sigma_D\big(c_\pi^* \bar S\big) = c_\pi^*\big(\sigma_{D/\pi}(\bar S)\big), \qquad
> \sigma_{D'}\big(S|_{D'}\big) = \sigma_D(S)\big|_{D'}, \qquad
> \sigma_{D_1 \times D_2}(S_1 \sqcup S_2) = \sigma_{D_1}(S_1) \times \sigma_{D_2}(S_2)
> $$
> (Lemma 83.2 applied to joins of kernels), so registered content is functorial along restriction, refinement, and coarsening, and the adjunction of Theorem 6.2 transports: along a coarsening the entire T1 package restricts to the ideal ${\downarrow}\pi$; along products it factorizes. Moreover the focusing operator of Proposition 6.11 acquires its structural identity: $s_\rho$, the least $\rho$-saturated superset, **is the reflector** — the left adjoint of the inclusion $\Phi_\rho \hookrightarrow \mathcal{P}(D)$ — so *extraction is itself a left adjoint*, joining $\sigma$ in the manuscript's adjunction discipline. Admissible content inherits the calculus with one caveat inherited from Lemma 83.2(2): ceilings involving meets transport exactly along coarsenings and products and only laxly along restrictions — restricting a scene can *sever* admissible identifications that chained through excluded candidates. $\square$

> **Definition 83.4 (the global algebra).** Let $\mathcal{D}$ be generated from a joint scene domain $D^*$ by coarsenings and products (the "one scene, many questions, many targets" case; restrictions are treated as morphisms below rather than as generators). The **label lattice** $L \subseteq \mathrm{Part}(D^*)$ is the sublattice generated by the $c^*$-pullbacks of the achievable-quotient lattices $\mathrm{Ach}(V_D)$, $D \in \mathcal{D}$; the **global pieces** are the labeled pairs
> $$
> \Phi^{\mathcal{D}} = \bigsqcup_{\rho \in L} \Phi_\rho, \qquad \Phi_\rho = \{(E, \rho) : E \subseteq D^* \ \rho\text{-saturated}\},
> $$
> with $d, \otimes, \Rightarrow$ as in Proposition 6.11 over $L$ — labels composing as there: $d(E,\rho) = \rho$, combination intersects sets and joins labels, focusing saturates and relabels — and **vacuous extension** of a piece of a member domain given by $c^*$-pullback of its saturated sets.

> **Theorem 83.5 (H5 discharged: the multi-domain information algebra).**
> 1. $(\Phi^{\mathcal{D}}, L; d, \otimes, \Rightarrow)$ is a **labeled idempotent information algebra** with support: Proposition 6.11's proofs apply verbatim with $\mathrm{Ach}(V)$ replaced by any $\vee$-closed label family, vacuous extension commutes exactly with combination and focusing ($s_{c^*\rho}(c^*\varphi) = c^*(s_\rho \varphi)$, by surjectivity of $c$ and Lemma 83.2(1)), and every piece has a least supporting label ($\varphi$ saturated for $\rho$ and $\rho'$ is saturated for $\rho \wedge \rho'$ — alternating chains stay inside $\varphi$ stepwise — so the meet of supporting labels supports).
> 2. **(Nontrivial extraction.)** For any question over any member domain, extraction of "what the corpus says about it" is the focusing $\varphi^{\Rightarrow \rho}$ through $L$; the combination of all per-view pieces of all member domains, focused across $\mathcal{D}$, is "what the corpus says about anything" — (A5)'s deferred parenthetical, now an operation of the algebra.
> 3. **(Restrictions act laxly.)** The trace $\varphi \mapsto \varphi \cap D'$ defines morphisms that are **exact for combination** ($(\varphi \cap \psi)|_{D'} = \varphi|_{D'} \cap \psi|_{D'}$) and **lax for focusing**, with direction $s_{\rho|}(\varphi|) \subseteq (s_\rho \varphi)|$ — a block may meet $\varphi$ only outside $D'$ — the algebra-level face of Lemma 83.2(2).
> 4. **(Single-domain fiber.)** For $\mathcal{D} = \{D\}$ the construction is Proposition 6.11 verbatim (Theorem 88.1 states this as the last recovery).
>
> *Proof.* 1: labeling, transitivity, and the combination axiom are Proposition 6.11's three arguments, which used of $\mathrm{Ach}(V)$ only $\vee$-closure; the vacuous-extension commutations are the displayed identities, each a one-line check via preimages ($c$ surjective: a pulled-back block meets $c^*\varphi$ iff its image block meets $\varphi$); support as shown. 2: instance of the operations. 3: intersection commutes with trace; the focusing laxity and its strictness are Lemma 83.2(2) at the piece level. 4: by inspection. $\square$

> **Remark 83.6 (representability, one last time).** The ideal completion of Remark 24.6 commutes with the whole construction — down-sets pull back and trace — so the representability coordinate is stable under the multi-domain extension, and the graded algebra of §84 has its ideal-valued repair available at the usual price. Fourth and final appearance of the trade-off; no new mechanism.

## 84. The algebra coordinate

> **Theorem 84.1 (the fate of the algebra axioms; accumulation retro-named).** Track Kohlas's axioms across the manuscript's regimes, combination of graded pieces being the product of channels under the family's coupling (two looks at once):
> 1. **(Exact.)** Labeled idempotent information algebra, single- and multi-domain (Proposition 6.11, Theorem 83.5): all axioms, idempotency included — because exact combination is a lattice join, and joins are idempotent.
> 2. **(Graded: idempotency fails — accumulation is its failure.)** For a noisy view $N$, $\varphi \otimes \varphi = N \otimes N \succ_B N = \varphi$ strictly (Proposition 23.3's posterior computation; certified here also by linear program, $\delta(N, N \otimes N) = 0.084$ at crossover $0.3$, while $\delta(N \otimes N, N) = 0$). The graded pieces thus form at best a **valuation algebra that is not an information algebra** — and since Kohlas's literature draws exactly this line (information algebras *are* the idempotent valuation algebras [Kohlas 2003; Kohlas 2017; Shenoy & Shafer 1990]), the manuscript's accumulation phenomenon (§23.1), the force behind (K3̂) and its expressive trade-off, **is the failure of the idempotency axiom, under its classical name**. The exact/graded boundary of the whole manuscript is Kohlas's information/valuation boundary.
> 3. **(Graded: the remaining axioms stratify as the content maps did.)** At the statistical stratum over CI families the labeling and combination laws hold (Proposition 22.4(3)); coupling breaks them off the CI class (22.4(2)); at the full stratum combination of Blackwell classes is not even well defined — the coupling fiber again (Propositions 22.5, 39.3) — with the ideal-valued repair of Remark 83.6 restoring operations at the price of representability.
>
> The verdicts are re-organizations of proved theorems, and the theorem's content is the organization: **algebra** joins codomain, compositionality, representability, fidelity, and descent as the sixth tracked coordinate, and each of its degradations already had a part of its own. $\square$

> **Proposition 84.2 (admissibility as a subalgebra condition).** Let $K$ be monotone and consider the admissible labeled family $T \mapsto \gamma_K(T)$ as a content system (Definition 24.3). The admissible pieces form a **labeled subalgebra** of the exact algebra — the label assignment closed under the combination law $d(\varphi \otimes \psi) = d(\varphi) \vee d(\psi)$ within admissible labels — iff the system's comparison cells are invertible, i.e. iff $K$ is **separable** (Definition 24.5's gluing; Corollary 15.4). At non-separable $K$, combining admissible pieces of $T$ and $T'$ produces the label $\gamma_K(T) \vee \gamma_K(T')$, which no admissible reading of $T \cup T'$ is confined to and which falls short of $\gamma_K(T \cup T')$ by exactly the anomaly interval $\Delta_K(T \cup T')$-side data: **the registration anomaly is the combination-closure defect of the admissible subalgebra**, the algebra-typed face of the same fact whose adjunction face is Corollary 15.4 and whose descent face is Theorem 37.3. $\square$

The theorem's reading, stated once and plainly: the framework does not replace classical information theory; it exhibits classical information theory as the maximally structured fiber of a larger, mostly untamed space, and it prices every step away from that fiber — in lattice structure lost, in bits, in deficiency, in error mass, in forged provenance. The kernel is where all the pathologies are invisible; the manuscript is the map of where each one becomes visible first, what it costs, and which dial moves it.

## 86. Named open problems

The completion criterion of §73 requires the residue to be retired precisely. Seven problems, each with its provenance.

**P1 (PID inside one address).** Does the witness class admit an internal nonnegative decomposition graded by the missing-face dimension of the nerve hierarchy (§38.3), i.e. a partial information decomposition *within* a single descent address, where Theorem 56.2's transport obstruction cannot arise because the ledger is fixed? Provenance: Remark 57.2.

**P2 (topos-level coverings).** The union coverage is Grothendieck and the determination coverage is not (Lemma 35.2); the obstruction calculus was built site-free. Is there a topos-theoretic home — a subcanonical refinement of the determination coverage, or a change of base — in which Part V's descent theory is the internal sheaf condition? Provenance: Lemma 35.2, Part V §43.

**P3 (sheafification of Theorem 6.7).** Whether registration-plus-consolidation is a sheafification in a precise sense — the universal property of Theorem 6.7 as the unit of a sheafification adjunction over the H4 site. Provenance: §8's H4 clause; open since Part I.

**P4 (used content).** A model of the downstream process that *uses* registered content, coarsening "extractable" to "used"; the evaluation functional's $(\mu, q)$-dependence (§53) is the flagged entry point — *used* is plausibly *evaluated against the questions the process actually asks*. Provenance: Part II §17; §59.

**P5 (the unified graded-ceiling chain).** Theorem 57.1's exact and graded clauses glue along the deterministic fiber but are not one identity, and Part IX's half-gap (Proposition 69.2) is the stated reason to expect none of the obvious unifications to work: the two clauses live at metrically separated scales. The algebra of §84 does not dissolve the problem (the clauses differ precisely where idempotency does). A single chain, if it exists, must bridge the gap the enriched rung exposes. Provenance: §59, §73, §81.

**P6 (infinite horizon; continuous time).** The dynamical theory of Part X is complete at finite horizon under (A6); its infinite-horizon and continuous-time forms belong with the measure-theoretic base of Appendix A and are genuinely open — in particular whether the causal gap and the detection exponent have stationary-process forms. Provenance: §81.

**P7 (local computation).** The one problem this part creates. The exact algebra of Theorem 83.5 satisfies the axioms under which Shenoy–Shafer propagation organizes inference by message passing on join trees [Shenoy & Shafer 1990; Kohlas 2003]. Does the framework's *full* structure propagate? Concretely: what do non-idempotency (accumulation), coupling fibers, and admissibility ceilings do to local-computation architectures — is there a message-passing calculus whose messages are interval anomalies and whose fixed points are the stratified decomposition's addresses? Provenance: §§83–84.

## 87. Worked example M: one corpus, many domains

The laboratory, final appearance. Its corpus of records now serves two clients over one ambient class: an insurer running **entity resolution** — contrast domain $D_1$, identity reading, candidates are possible identities of the file's subject — and a clinic running **state tracking** — $D_2$, a coarsening of the joint scene domain $D^*$ by the question "which disease stage," tracked-target reading. The global algebra of Theorem 83.5 is the corpus's account of both at once: each record contributes a piece labeled in $L \subseteq \mathrm{Part}(D^*)$; the insurer's answer is a focusing $\varphi^{\Rightarrow \rho_1}$, the clinic's a focusing through the pulled-back stage question; and "everything the corpus says about anything" — the phrase (A5) deferred on page one — is the combination of all pieces, an element of the algebra with a least supporting label. Part VII's ledger discipline is now geography: the clinic's coarse ledger *is* the coarsening morphism $c_{\ker q}$, and the transport theorem (56.2) says the insurer and the clinic may correctly file the same information quantum at different addresses — not a paradox but a change of domain. Two of the part's caveats appear in operation. The clinic re-runs an assay and books the repeat as confirmatory; the algebra books it as $\varphi \otimes \varphi \succ_B \varphi$ — non-idempotent combination, accumulation by its classical name, and the reason the lab's (K3̂)-closed ceilings were priced as they were (Remark 23.6). And the insurer restricts to a sub-cohort $D' \subseteq D_1$ for an audit: two files that the full corpus admissibly identified fall apart in the restriction, because their identifying chain ran through an excluded third file — Lemma 83.2(2)'s laxity, live: **restriction can sever what combination had joined, and the severance is not an error but a theorem.**

## 88. The last recovery, and the coda

> **Theorem 88.1 (the (A5) fiber).** For the singleton family $\mathcal{D} = \{D\}$, the global algebra of Theorem 83.5 is Proposition 6.11 verbatim: $L = \mathrm{Ach}(V)$, vacuous extension is trivial, restriction morphisms are absent, and the labeled idempotent information algebra over the achievable-quotient lattice is recovered exactly. The multi-domain theory collapses onto its page-one germ, as each relaxation before it collapsed onto its own. $\square$

**Coda.** The manuscript set out to say what information *over a contrast domain* is, and found that the saying had a shape: a kernel where content is a complete lattice, a left adjoint, and an idempotent algebra — and around it, in every direction the assumptions permit, a controlled collapse. Bounded decoding costs join-preservation and leaves an anomaly with two named components. Noise costs idempotency, splits content into strata, and hands unions over to couplings that the parts do not determine. Anchoring costs the identity of record and world, and prices the difference in error mass at Fano's rate. Corruption costs comparison itself, at thresholds now computed. Time costs nothing and pays twice — to the adaptive interpreter and to the committed record. Multiplicity of domains costs the invariance of addresses. Each cost was measured three times — in lattice structure, in bits, in deficiency — and each measurement had its own arithmetic: the exact calculus composes, the Shannon evaluation telescopes because it is a coboundary, the enriched evaluation obeys only the triangle inequality, and the failure of the better laws is in every case a theorem with an exhibit. The promises the text made along the way are, as of this part, all kept or all named: the hooks H1–H5 discharged, the assumptions (A2)–(A5), (A7) each relaxed once, (A1) held by design, (A6) reinterpreted in the appendix, the residue standing in §86 as seven problems with their provenances. What remains at the center is the statement the collapse theorem makes precise: classical information theory is not what this framework revises but where this framework is *smooth* — the fiber on which every obstruction vanishes — and the theory of registered information is the geometry of leaving it.
