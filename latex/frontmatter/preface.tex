\markboth{Preface}{Preface}
This book develops a theory of how raw presentations of things become information about those things, relative to questions. It was written constructively: the first part builds the smallest closed mathematical system in which the central object can be defined and its basic theorems proved exactly, and every later part is a controlled relaxation of a named assumption of that system.

\subsection*{How the book is organized}
Parts~I--III are the foundations: the deterministic kernel, worked examples with the admissibility layer, and the anchoring interface with the graded theory. Parts~IV--VI treat the substantive theory of anchoring, descent and obstruction, and composite relaxations. Parts~VII and~IX are the quantitative parts; Parts~VIII and~X concern corruption and dynamical scenes. Part~XI is the qualified synthesis. The appendices contain the measurable extensions and their limits (A), computational certificates (B), a controlled vocabulary with a guide to notation (C), and supplementary finite theorems (D).

Section~8 of Part~I explains precisely how the later parts relate to the kernel: each either generalizes one of its primitives and proves that the kernel is recovered as a special case, or asks a new question of the layers already built. It also lists every standing assumption of the book, with the part that relaxes it. Each later part opens with an overview and closes with a summary of what it establishes and what it leaves open.

\subsection*{Conventions}
Sections are numbered continuously through the book, so that ``\S$n$'' names a unique section and statements carry the number of the section that contains them: Theorem~$n.m$ is the $m$-th numbered statement of \S$n$. Definitions, theorems, propositions, lemmas, corollaries, remarks and facts share one counter. Appendix statements are numbered A.1, B.1, \dots, D.13. Unless a statement says otherwise, all state and observation spaces are finite and nonempty. Citations follow the author--year system; the bibliography is at the end of the book.

\subsection*{Status of the results}
Statements, proofs and scope conditions are authoritative as printed. Several broader claims one might expect are deliberately \emph{not} claimed; they are listed as named open problems in \S86, and Appendix~A explains which measurable generalizations remain to be proved.
