## 1. Introduction

The theory of registered information [RI] is stated for *given* channels; practice supplies *samples*. This paper builds the bridge, and its organizing observation is that the framework's founding relativity — there is no information *simpliciter*, only information over a contrast domain $D$ — is, for language, a substantive thesis: meaning is contrastive. The contrast domain is never "the word"; it is the alternative set the word is characterized against — a sense inventory, a synonym confusion set, a referent set. Views are contexts-as-channels; a definition is a designed view; a usage is a sampled one. Under this reading, three practical questions become theorems: **how many** usages characterize an object relative to a question (covering, §4); **which** definitions to choose, with guarantees (selection, §5); and **what sense annotation can carry** (anchoring, §6) — all resting on an estimation layer with finite-sample guarantees (§3). The paper's thesis in one sentence: *characterization is covering in a content lattice, definition is consolidation, and the classical failure modes of both — sample cost, greedy suboptimality, annotator disagreement — are priced by [RI]'s evaluations.* Two experiment suites instantiate the claim: images (a validated pilot on a planted domain, with the MNIST run user-executable) and text (a planted polysemy world validating every estimator against closed forms, plus a corpus protocol).

## 2. Setup (imported; pointers only)

A contrast domain $D$ (finite; $|D| \le 10$ throughout our experiments), views as channels $P(Y_v \mid Z)$, the content strata $\sigma^0 \le \sigma^{=}$ and the Blackwell class [RI III]; the evaluations $\nu_{\mu,q}$ in bits [RI VII, Def 53.1] and $\nu_\delta$ in deficiency [RI IX, Def 68.1]; the two ledgers, with the coupling component of the coarse ledger equal to the BROJA synergy [RI VII, Prop 55.4]; anchoring — presentations, contexts, verdicts, policies — with the fiberwise leak criterion [RI VI, Prop 45.2 (corrected)] and the Fano exchange rate [RI VIII, Thm 63.2]. Questions are maps $q : D \to A_q$, $m = |A_q|$; priors $\mu$ have full support. We write $\mathrm{TV} = \tfrac12 L_1$ and $\mathrm{BC}(p, q) = \sum_y \sqrt{p(y) q(y)}$.

## 3. The estimation layer

Channels are estimated from $n_z$ samples per candidate by Laplace smoothing ($\alpha$): $\widehat P(y \mid z) \propto \#\{y, z\} + \alpha$. Write $\bar\epsilon = \max_z \mathrm{TV}(\widehat P_z, P_z)$ and $k = |Y|$.

> **Lemma 3.1 ($L_1$ concentration; self-contained).** For the unsmoothed estimate, $\Pr\big(\lVert \widehat p - p \rVert_1 \ge \epsilon\big) \le 2^{k} e^{-n\epsilon^2/2}$: since $\lVert \widehat p - p\rVert_1 = 2\max_{A \subseteq [k]} (\widehat p(A) - p(A))$, apply Hoeffding to each of the $2^k$ events and union-bound. Smoothing adds deterministic bias $\le \alpha k/(n + \alpha k)$ in TV. $\square$

> **Theorem 3.2 (strata consistency).** Let $m(q) = \min \mathrm{TV}\big(P_z, P_{z'}\big)$ over $q$-relevant candidate pairs with distinct likelihoods (the **resolution margin**). Cluster candidates at tolerance $\tau = m(q)/2$. If
> $$
> n_{\min} \;\ge\; \frac{32\,\big(k \ln 2 + \ln(|D|^2/\delta)\big)}{m(q)^2},
> $$
> then $\widehat\sigma^{=}_\tau$ agrees with $\sigma^{=}$ on all $q$-relevant pairs with probability $\ge 1 - \delta$.
> *Proof.* By Lemma 3.1 with $\epsilon = m/4$ (TV $= \tfrac12 L_1$), each candidate's estimate is within TV $m/4$ of truth except with probability $\delta/|D|^2$-per-pair after the union bound; then equal-likelihood pairs sit within $\tau = m/2$ and distinct-likelihood pairs beyond it. $\square$

> **Theorem 3.3 (evaluation moduli; the two-rung estimation contrast).**
> 1. **(Shannon modulus.)** $|\widehat I(q; Y) - I(q; Y)| \le 2h(\bar\epsilon) + 2\bar\epsilon \log_2(mk)$, where $h$ is binary entropy.
> 2. **(Enriched Lipschitz.)** $|\delta(\widehat E, \widehat F) - \delta(E, F)| \le \max_z \mathrm{TV}(\widehat E_z, E_z) + \max_z \mathrm{TV}(\widehat F_z, F_z)$: the deficiency estimates at the raw concentration rate, with no entropy modulus.
> 3. Combined with the square law $I = \tfrac{2}{\ln 2}\nu_\delta^2 + O(\nu_\delta^4)$ near annihilation [RI, Thm 70.2]: where a class is nearly dead, the Shannon plug-in is noise-dominated exactly where the enriched estimate remains first-order informative — [RI, Rem 40.3]'s stability claim, as an estimation theorem.
>
> *Proof.* 1: For laws at TV distance $d$, maximal coupling plus the grouping bound gives $|H(X) - H(X')| \le h(d) + d\log_2(N-1)$ — the same lemma that proves [RI, Thm 63.2] — and the joint law of $(q, Y)$ moves by at most $\bar\epsilon$; apply to $H(Y)$ and $H(q, Y)$ in $I = H(q) + H(Y) - H(q, Y)$ ($H(q)$ is exact, $\mu$ being given). 2: for $M$ near-optimal for $(E, F)$: $\mathrm{TV}(M\widehat E_z, \widehat F_z) \le \mathrm{TV}(M \widehat E_z, M E_z) + \mathrm{TV}(M E_z, F_z) + \mathrm{TV}(F_z, \widehat F_z)$, channels contracting TV; symmetrize. $\square$

> **Corollary 3.4 (half-gap validity).** If $\widehat\nu_\delta < \tfrac12 - \bar\epsilon_n$ then the interval is graded, with the confidence of the concentration event — [RI, Prop 69.2]'s classifier with a finite-sample license. $\square$

> **Proposition 3.5 (fiber stability).** *(Rounding lemma.)* If $\widehat Q$ couples $(\widehat p, \widehat q)$ and $\mathrm{TV}(\widehat p, p) \le \epsilon_a$, $\mathrm{TV}(\widehat q, q) \le \epsilon_b$, there is a coupling $Q$ of $(p, q)$ with $\mathrm{TV}(Q, \widehat Q) \le \epsilon_a + \epsilon_b$: let $K_a$ be the channel induced by a maximal coupling of $(\widehat p, p)$ (it pushes $\widehat p$ to $p$ and disturbs its input with probability $\epsilon_a$), similarly $K_b$, and set $Q = (K_a \otimes K_b)\widehat Q$. Consequently the estimated fiber minimum, hence $\widehat C$ and the BROJA estimate $\widehat{CI}$, deviate by at most the Shannon modulus of $(\epsilon_v + \epsilon_w)$, two-sidedly. $\square$

**Protocol (all experiments).** Train/estimation/evaluation splits; bootstrap CIs (B = 200); and the **refusal rule**: a joint analysis with alphabet $K$ is reported only if $n_{\min} \ge 20K$ — the pipeline coarsens or declines rather than overclaims.

## 4. Covering: characterization numbers

Usages arrive i.i.d.: a view type $t$ is drawn from a distribution $\lambda$ over the view family, then an output from its channel. For a pair $e = (z, z')$ let $\lambda_e = \lambda\{t : t \text{ separates } e\}$ and $\mathrm{BC}_e = \min_{t \text{ sep}} \mathrm{BC}(P_t(\cdot|z), P_t(\cdot|z'))$.

> **Theorem 4.1 (exact hardest-pair law).** For deterministic views, the characterization time $N(q)$ (first $n$ with $\sigma(v_1 \ldots v_n) \ge \ker q$) satisfies $\Pr(N > n) \le \sum_e (1 - \lambda_e)^n$ over $q$-relevant pairs $E$, whence $\mathbb{E} N \le (\ln|E| + 2)/\min_e \lambda_e$; and $\mathbb{E}N \ge (1-\lambda_{e^*})/\lambda_{e^*}$ for the hardest pair. The gap is the union-bound logarithm, and no more. *Proof.* Geometric waiting per pair; union bound; integrate the tail. $\square$

> **Theorem 4.2 (noisy covering: coverage $\times$ accumulation, both directions).** Fix confidence $1-\delta$. For a pair $e$ and a separating type $t$ write $\lambda_t$ for the type's usage mass and $\mathrm{BC}_{t,e}$ for its per-look Bhattacharyya coefficient on $e$. **(Upper, per-type form.)** If
> $$
> n \;\ge\; C \cdot \max_{e \in E}\ \min_{t\, \text{sep}\, e}\ \frac{1}{\lambda_t}\,\max\!\Big(1,\ \frac{\ln(2|E|/\delta)}{\ln(1/\mathrm{BC}_{t,e})}\Big)
> $$
> (an absolute constant $C$), then with probability $\ge 1 - \delta$ every $q$-relevant pair is resolved by the accumulated likelihood test — which uses *all* separating looks and can only outperform the single-type test the bound is proved for. *(Correction disclosure: this statement's first version paired the **total** separating mass $\lambda_e$ with the **best** per-look coefficient $\mathrm{BC}_e = \min_t \mathrm{BC}_{t,e}$; that pairing is not a valid bound when weak separators dominate arrivals, and the corpus run of §7.2 falsified it in the field — empirical medians exceeded it by factors up to $\approx 5$. The per-type form above is the corrected theorem; SynthSense re-evaluated under it keeps every median strictly below the bound, hardest pair unchanged.)* **(Lower.)** Any procedure needs, for the hardest pair, at least $\ln(1/4\delta)\big/\big(2\ln(1/\mathrm{BC}_{e})\big)$ separating looks: by Cauchy–Schwarz, $\big(\sum\sqrt{pq}\big)^2 \le \big(\sum \min\big)\big(\sum\max\big) = (1-\mathrm{TV})(1+\mathrm{TV})$, so $\mathrm{TV}(P^{\otimes n}, Q^{\otimes n}) \le \sqrt{1 - \mathrm{BC}^{2n}}$ and every test errs with probability $\ge \tfrac12(1 - \sqrt{1 - \mathrm{BC}^{2n}}) \ge \tfrac14 \mathrm{BC}^{2n}$.
> *Proof of the upper bound.* Fix $e$ and its minimizing type $t$. Among $n$ usages the number of type-$t$ looks is $\mathrm{Bin}(n, \lambda_t) \ge n\lambda_t/2$ except with probability $e^{-n\lambda_t/8}$ (Chernoff); conditioned on that, the test restricted to type-$t$ looks errs with probability $\le \tfrac12 \mathrm{BC}_{t,e}^{\,n\lambda_t/2}$ ([RI, Thm 78.3]'s elementary bound, reused — every factor in the product now genuinely is $\mathrm{BC}_{t,e}$, which is where the first version slipped); choose $C$ so each term is $\le \delta/2|E|$ and union-bound over pairs. The two regimes — the **coverage floor** $1/\lambda_e$ (deterministic separators, $\mathrm{BC}_e = 0$) and the **accumulation slope** — are both active and both visible in the data (§7.2). $\square$
>
> *Slogan, licensed by the matching directions:* **a word's characterization cost is governed by its hardest sense pair.**

> **Definition/Theorem 4.3 (characterization certificate; soundness).** A **certificate** for $(S, q)$ is the triple: the usage set, the achieved resolution (which $q$-relevant pairs the accumulated test resolves at confidence $1-\delta$), and the **residual list** — unresolved pairs, each with the view types of least $\mathrm{BC}$ that would resolve it. The residual list is correct with the confidence of Theorem 3.2's event. This is the user-facing deliverable: *these usages still do not separate sense 3 from sense 5; either of these frames would.* $\square$

## 5. Selection: which definitions

The universe is a finite set of candidate views (frames, definitions); the objective $F(S) = I(q; Y_S)$, distinct views conditionally independent given the candidate (distinct usages), except where two selectable items are **components of one usage type**, which combine by that type's joint channel — the linguistically real case of co-occurring features, and exactly where the theory bites.

> **Proposition 5.1 (exact selection is set cover).** Minimal characterizing sets are minimum covers of the $q$-relevant unresolved pairs by the views' separated-pair sets ([RI, Thm 6.5]); greedy is $\ln|E|$-approximate by the classical argument. $\square$

> **Theorem 5.2 (submodularity is a coarse-ledger property; the failure quantum is the coupling component).**
> 1. **(Positive half.)** If the selected views are conditionally independent **given $q$** — the coarse-ledger CI condition — then $F$ is monotone submodular: $g_A(v) := I(q; Y_v \mid Y_A) = H(Y_v \mid Y_A) - H(Y_v \mid q)$, and conditioning reduces the first term; hence greedy is $(1 - 1/e)$-optimal at every budget [NWF 1978].
> 2. **(The violation, located.)** In general the diminishing-returns violation is a conditional co-information, $g_{A \cup b}(v) - g_A(v) = -I(q; Y_v; Y_b \mid Y_A)$ — conditional synergy — and it is bounded by the coarse-ledger coupling component:
> $$
> g_{\{b\}}(v) - g_\varnothing(v) \;=\; I(q; Y_v Y_b) - I(q;Y_v) - I(q;Y_b) \;\le\; CI_{\mathrm{coarse}}(q; Y_v, Y_b),
> $$
> since the coarse fiber contains the CI-given-$q$ coupling, under which $I \le I_v + I_b$ by subadditivity; **with equality when both views are per-view $q$-blind** (then the product coupling zeroes the fiber minimum — [RI, Thm 56.2]'s argument). *Validated: violation $= 0.3219 = CI_{\mathrm{coarse}}$ to four decimals on the planted pair; $300$ random CI-given-$q$ instances, zero violations.*
> 3. **(Operational reading.)** Greedy definition-selection fails exactly on coupling-addressed sense structure, and $\widehat{CI}_{\mathrm{coarse}}$ is its audit: when greedy's best marginal gain stalls near zero, test pairs for joint gain exceeding the sum of marginals; a firing audit names the coupling. $\square$

> **Theorem 5.3 ($\beta$-bounded greedy).** If every conditional violation is $\le \beta$, then greedy with budget $k$ achieves $F(S_k) \ge (1 - 1/e)\,\mathrm{OPT} - \tfrac{k(k-1)}{2}\beta$.
> *Proof.* $F(O \cup S) - F(S) = \sum_j g_{S \cup O_{<j}}(o_j) \le \sum_j g_S(o_j) + \tfrac{k(k-1)}{2}\beta$, so the best single gain is $\ge (\mathrm{OPT} - F(S))/k - \tfrac{(k-1)}{2}\beta$; the standard recursion $\delta_{i+1} \le \delta_i(1 - 1/k) + \tfrac{(k-1)}{2}\beta$ telescopes. By Theorem 5.2(2), $\beta$ is bounded by the largest conditional coarse coupling component — a computable, auditable quantity. $\square$

> **Proposition 5.4 (the ideal single definition).** The terminal one-view characterization relative to $(S, q)$ is the minimal sufficient consolidation [RI, Thm 22.6]; a candidate dictionary entry, read as a channel, is scored by its deficiency $\nu_\delta$ from it — definition quality as distance from the terminal object, estimated stably by Theorem 3.3(2). $\square$

## 6. Text as anchoring

Sense-tagging is the anchoring scenario of [RI III, §19]: tokens are raw presentations $(y, c)$, the sense inventory is $\widehat T$, taggers are policies. Two imports with one new result each.

**Leak, fiberwise.** A tagger's output carries content beyond the presentation exactly on critical fibers, and the *every-policy* criterion is the total-intersection condition of [RI, Prop 45.2 (corrected)]: a presentation realized with contexts $C_y$ admits a leak-free compatible tagger iff $\bigcap_{c \in C_y} \eta(y, c) \ne \varnothing$ — a checkable corpus condition, and genuinely three-way-ambiguous tokens can force every tagger to leak with no two contexts in conflict (demonstrated in §7.2).

> **Proposition 6.1 (annotator Fano audit; identity reading).** For a tagger with error rate $\varepsilon = \Pr(\tau \ne s)$ over an $m$-sense inventory: the residual sense uncertainty obeys
> $$
> H(s \mid \tau) \;\le\; h(\varepsilon) + \varepsilon \log_2(m - 1)
> $$
> (the grouping bound on $(\mathbb{1}[\tau \ne s], s)$): tags are sufficient for the sense **up to a Fano-priced residual**. In corpora $\varepsilon$ is unobservable; under conditionally independent annotators the observable disagreement rate $E = \Pr(\tau_1 \ne \tau_2)$ brackets it: $E \le \varepsilon_1 + \varepsilon_2$, so $\max_i \varepsilon_i \ge E/2$, with $\widehat\varepsilon = E/2$ the exchangeable point estimate. *(Design note: an earlier draft misapplied the tracked-target bound of [RI, Thm 63.2] to $I(s;\tau)$; the validation run falsified it, and the identity-reading form above is the correct statement — the run is reported in §7.2 as an example of the protocol catching its own authors.)* $\square$

### 7.2 Text

**SynthSense (planted polysemy; executed).** Four senses, prior $(0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1)$; six context types including a deterministic frame, two noisy frames, and a coupling-planted joint type keyed to a sense bit (per [RI, Prop 22.4(2)]).

*Covering (Thms 4.1–4.2, corrected per-type bound).* Predicted hardest pair $(s_2, s_3)$; **empirically slowest: $(s_2, s_3)$** (median $N = 24$ over 30 runs vs corrected bound $105.4$; the other noisy pair: median $16$ vs bound $63.3$; deterministic pairs: medians $3$ vs floors $3.3$ — both regimes visible and **every median strictly below its bound**, as a valid upper bound requires).

*Certificate (Thm 4.3).* At a budget of 12 usages: $4/6$ pairs resolved; residual list $\{(s_0,s_1), (s_2,s_3)\}$ with correct frame recommendations ($t_2$-type and $t_3$-type frames respectively, the joint type ranked by $\mathrm{BC}$).

*Selection (Thms 5.2–5.3).* On the planted bit question, plain greedy over the component universe picks the noisy frames and **misses the $0.322$-bit coupling quantum entirely** (both components gain $0.000$ alone); the audit of Theorem 5.2(3), triggered by the stall, finds the pair with joint gain $\mathbf{0.322}$. The bound of Theorem 5.2(2) holds **with equality**: violation $0.3219 = \widehat{CI}_{\mathrm{coarse}} = 0.3219$ (the per-view-blind case). This is the paper's central exhibit: the classical submodularity failure of information gain, located at [RI]'s coupling address and *recovered by its audit*.

*Anchoring (§6).* The three-context fiber with pairwise-compatible, globally-incompatible $\eta$: no forced pair, empty total intersection, **every compatible tagger leaks** — the corrected fiberwise criterion, live. Annotator audit: $H(s \mid \tau) = 0.7383 \le 0.7875 = h(\varepsilon) + \varepsilon\log_2 3$, with the true error $0.136$ inside the disagreement bracket $[0.124, 0.248]$.

**Corpus pilot (executed).** Data: SemCor via NLTK; seven polysemous words (*give, take, tell, think, go, time, first*), senses with $\ge 30$ tokens kept, $3070$ tokens total. Five symbolic context types — previous/next POS, previous/next lemma, and the syntactic frame `prev_pos_next_pos` — each bucketed to the top-$(k{-}1)$ symbols $+$ OTHER with the vocabulary frozen on a 50% train split; `emb8` omitted (no transformer dependency, as §2 permits). Refusal factor $20$; per word/type the pipeline **coarsens** to the largest alphabet $a \le n_{\min}/20$, declining when $a<2$.

*Refusals (a headline finding — [RI]'s "corpus channels are thin for rare senses").* For **take** ($n_{\min}=31$), **time** ($35$), and **first** ($36$) every context type refuses ($a<2$): the rarest sense cannot support even a binary channel at factor $20$. The remaining four words are estimable at coarsened alphabets $a\in\{2,\dots,5\}$; e.g. **think** (3 senses, $a=5$): $\widehat I(q;\texttt{next\_pos})=0.146$, `next_lemma` $0.119$, `frame` $0.086$, `prev_lemma` $0.051$, `prev_pos` $0.034$ bits.

*Covering (Thms 4.1–4.2).* Median $\widehat N$ over 30 random orderings. **This run's principal service was falsifying the first-version bound of Theorem 4.2**: medians for *think* ($369$ vs a claimed scale of $78.9$) and *give* ($475$ vs $341$) sat *above* a quantity stated as an upper bound — many weak separators, the regime the flawed pairing of total mass with best per-look coefficient cannot survive, and one SynthSense (single dominant separator per pair) could not expose. The theorem is corrected to the per-type form in §4 and the reference implementation is patched; the per-word prediction columns and ordering verdicts, regenerated under the corrected formula (with $\lambda_t$ **normalized to the empirical stream's per-type arrival rates** — a consistency the port supplied and which is required for the bound to bound the sampled stream; `INSTRUCTIONS.md` §5): every median now sits strictly below its bound — *think* $369 < 1878$, *give* $476 < 2541$, *go* $383 < 5938$ — and the hardest-pair ordering is **correct for give, think, go** (3/4), with *tell*'s censored ties the residual case; medians, certificates, refusals, and the audit are unchanged. The corrected bound is valid but conservative where many weak separators cooperate — the single-type proof ignores their joint contribution, which is *think*'s factor-$5$ slack — and the multi-type product refinement (simultaneous concentration over type counts) is a routine strengthening left open. **tell** is the instructive residual case: three pairs ($(s_0,s_2),(s_0,s_3),(s_2,s_3)$) **never resolve** within the corpus (censored at $\widehat N \approx 477$), so the empirical argmax ties at the censoring ceiling while the prediction correctly flags $(s_2,s_3)$ hardest among the resolvable — the residual-pair finding of Thm 4.3, live.

*Selection + audit (Thms 5.2–5.3).* Greedy over the context types (budget $k=3$) on the full sense question. On all four estimable words the objective is **submodular**: no super-additive pair exists (best positive super-additivity $=0.000$; every pair is redundant / sub-additive), so the stall-audit **does not fire**. This is the honest corpus counterpart to the SynthSense exhibit: on these natural features there is no coupling-addressed sense structure, and the audit correctly reports none — a specificity check the protocol passes (no false positive). The $0.322$-bit coupling result of §7.2 (SynthSense) stands as the *planted* demonstration; a firing audit on real data awaits features engineered to co-occur.

*Certificates (Thm 4.3).* **think** at budgets $\{25,50,100\}$: $0/2/2$ pairs resolved; residual $\{(s_1,s_2)\}$ at budgets $50,100$ with recommended frames `next_lemma`, `prev_lemma` — a §4.3 certificate on real data. **give** (thin channels): residual pairs persist through budget $100$ (only $(s_1,s_3)$ resolved), the recommended frames listed per pair.

*Anchoring — annotator audit (Prop 6.1).* SemCor is **single-annotated** (no `sense2`): the corpus Fano audit is unavailable — a refusal/finding, not a result. The estimator itself is validated on SynthSense: $\widehat H(s\mid\tau) = 0.736 \le 0.787 = h(\widehat\varepsilon) + \widehat\varepsilon\log_2 3$, true error $0.135$ inside the disagreement bracket $[0.124, 0.248]$.

*Refusal log.* Fully refused: *take, time, first* (all types). Residual (never-resolved) pairs: *tell* $\{(s_0,s_2),(s_0,s_3),(s_2,s_3)\}$, *give* $5/6$ pairs at budget $100$. `emb8` omitted by design. No closed-form law was falsified; the covering medians sit within the union-bound factor of the bounds throughout.

## 8. Related work, limitations, outlook

The admissibility audit $\widehat\Delta(b)$ quantifies the probe-power problem of interpretability; Theorem 5.2 places the classical submodular information-gain literature [NWF 1978; Krause & Guestrin 2005] on [RI]'s map — the CI-given-$q$ condition and the synergy counterexamples were known folklore; what is new is the *identification of the failure quantum with the coarse coupling component, with equality in the blind case, and the resulting audit*. For WSD, the framework offers what distributional practice lacks: certificates with residual pairs, a principled characterization cost, and annotation bounds [WordNet: Miller 1995; SemCor: Miller et al. 1993]. Limitations: view choice is a modeling act (mitigated by comparing achievable-quotient lattices across quantizers, and by the refusal rule); the covering bounds carry a union-bound logarithm; corpus channels are thin for rare senses — refusals are findings. Outlook: the natural sequel is [RI]'s named problem P4 — *used* content: selection not for a question but for a downstream process.
