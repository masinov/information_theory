# The first stage of the reconstruction

The question is what must be admitted before an epistemic relation becomes possible. At this stage, the account needs to distinguish the structure that permits differentiation from the relation through which a differentiation could be registered. It does not yet need to explain the assessment of a judgement.

The reconstruction will use ordinary logical resources—identity, distinction, relation, and inference—to state its commitments. This does not attribute those resources to a knowing subject. A distinction made in the exposition is not automatically a distinction made by whatever the exposition describes.

## 1. Differentiability as the initial commitment

Let \(F\) designate what is under consideration. Calling it a *field* specifies the scope of the inquiry, without implying a spatial region, a collection of discrete objects, or something already presented to a subject. Nor does the notation establish a boundary intrinsic to reality. At this point, \(F\) identifies what the account concerns; any claim about its actual boundaries would require additional justification.

The initial commitment is:

> **\(F\) admits differentiation, without a particular differentiation having been selected.**

This requires clarification. It does not merely say that we can invent different descriptions of \(F\). It attributes to \(F\) a capacity to participate in a differentiated articulation, rather than leaving all differentiation attributable to the notation used by the theorist. It does not specify which articulation is possible, whether there is a uniquely preferred one, or whether its terms would be objects, qualities, regions, or something else.

Differentiability is consequently a modal commitment: it concerns what \(F\) admits. It does not require a collection of possible configurations to exist in a mental reservoir. Neither does it imply temporal change. The possibility of differentiating something and the possibility of its changing are different claims.

This commitment is not deduced from the mere existence of \(F\). Something’s being under consideration does not establish what structure it possesses. Differentiability is admitted because the proposed reconstruction concerns a differentiation attributable to its target, rather than one supplied entirely by an unrelated description. **It is a necessary condition for that route of explanation, not yet a sufficient account of epistemic possibility.**

No knowing subject has entered here. In particular, \(F\)’s admitting differentiation does not imply that anything differentiates it.

## 2. A possible differentiation is not an actual articulation

The next distinction is between the capacity just admitted and a particular articulation. From the claim that \(F\) admits differentiation, it does not follow that a differentiation has been realized.

When a particular articulation is considered, it can be described schematically as

\[
\langle a,\delta,b\rangle_F.
\]

Here \(a\) and \(b\) designate the differentiated terms, while \(\delta\) designates the respect or relation under which they are differentiated. The notation does not assume that \(a\) and \(b\) existed previously as independent objects. It distinguishes positions within the articulation being described.

The separating role of \(\delta\) is important, but it should not be converted into a third object situated between the other two. A spatial separator would require spatial organization. At this stage, \(\delta\) expresses the differentiation itself.

Nor must \(b\) be everything left over after \(a\) has been selected. That stronger interpretation would require an exhaustive partition of \(F\). A contrast between two terms and a division into “this” and “everything else within the field” are not equivalent constructions. The latter adds a requirement that every case within the scope belong to one side or the other.

These qualifications locate the first limit of the reconstruction: **differentiability permits articulation, but neither produces an actual articulation nor determines its form**. An account of a particular case must therefore supply the articulation it concerns rather than claim to have derived it from possibility alone.

Even once an articulation is specified, it remains an articulation of \(F\). Nothing so far establishes that it is registered elsewhere.

## 3. Why a receiving role must be introduced separately

If differentiation is to be registered, something must occupy a receiving role. Let \(R\) designate that role.

This is not yet the introduction of a person, a conscious subject, or an independently bounded physical system. \(R\) designates whatever bears the differentiation on the receiving side of the proposed relation. It might eventually be identified with an aspect of a larger organization that also includes \(F\); the distinction between roles does not establish two separate substances.

Nevertheless, some differentiation must be possible on the receiving side. Otherwise, the account could describe a contrast in \(F\) but nothing that distinguishes its registration from its non-registration. This requirement concerns the organization of the receiving condition, not the possession of concepts or classifications.

The need to distinguish these roles can be stated without assuming a complete theory of cognition. A differentiation in the target and a differentiation attributable to its reception are not the same fact. The target-side articulation could be present while no reception occurs. Conversely, the receiving side could possess differentiations unrelated to the target.

The existence of \(R\) therefore does not follow from the differentiability of \(F\). It is an additional commitment required when moving from an account of differentiation to an account of reception.

This also identifies how much individuation has been admitted. There must be enough to distinguish a target role from a receiving role within the proposed relation. There need not yet be enough to identify enduring objects or successive states of one continuing subject. ## 4. Differentiated sides do not establish a receptive relation

The combination of a differentiable \(F\) and a differentiable \(R\) still leaves their connection undetermined. They could be unrelated. A further relational commitment is therefore necessary.

Let \(C\) designate **receptive dependence**. Its minimal intended meaning is:

> **A specified differentiation of \(F\), through \(C\), determines a differentiation in the receiving condition of \(R\).**

“Determines” here introduces dependence, not mere resemblance. Two structures can resemble one another without either registering the other. Similarly, finding different receiving conditions alongside different target conditions does not, by itself, establish receptive dependence.

The distinction can be made more explicit by considering alternatives. Suppose a particular contrast in \(F\) has been specified. Relative to a fixed mode of receptivity and fixed background conditions, that contrast must make a difference to the receiving condition. If the receiving condition were entirely independent of the specified contrast, it would not register that contrast.

This is an additional modal commitment. The original claim of differentiability said only that a contrast was possible; receptive dependence says something about how a contrast on one side bears on differentiation on the other. It should not be hidden in an unexplained appeal to “systematic” or “relevant” coupling. Two limitations are essential.

First, the dependence has been **admitted**, not derived from the existence of differentiated terms. An independent relation cannot be obtained merely by describing its proposed participants. The justification for introducing \(C\) is that reception requires a connection not supplied by either participant alone.

Second, the direction of reception is part of the proposed relation; it cannot be read off from a pattern of matched distinctions. The same mathematical correspondence might be describable in either direction. A particular application must establish why one role is receptive to the other. Calling the correspondence a function would not establish that fact.

At the foundational level, therefore, receptive dependence remains an explicitly relational primitive. A causal explanation might later specify one way it is realized, but causality has not been silently included in the present definition.

## 5. What “registration” can mean at this stage

With these commitments in place, *registration* can be defined in a restricted structural sense:

> **A differentiation is registered when receptive dependence connects a specified target-side articulation with a differentiation of the receiving condition.**

The term adds no claim that the recipient understands, identifies, or judges anything. Those capacities have not been introduced.

This definition also avoids treating registration as the transfer of an object from one place to another. The differentiation on the receiving side need not resemble the target-side articulation or reproduce its organization. What is required is the specified dependence between them. Nor is a representational space implied: describing alternatives in the theory does not establish that the receiving side contains a space in which those alternatives are represented.

The resulting relation is not supplied by either side in isolation. Its target-side contribution is the articulation concerned; its receiving-side contribution is the differentiation through which that articulation is registered; its relational contribution is the dependence connecting them. Keeping these contributions distinct prevents the registration from being attributed to the target merely because the target is differentiated.

The target and receiver are thus roles within a relational structure. This does not yet explain the emergence of a knowing subject, but it identifies what would have to be elaborated before such an explanation could begin.

## 6. No temporal process has yet been derived

The preceding account concerns differentiation and dependence across specified alternatives. It has not described one condition occurring before another.

Accordingly, it cannot yet say that the receiving side *acquires* a differentiation, *retains* it, or *compares* it with a subsequent one. Each expression introduces temporal structure absent from the foundation as presently stated.

If reception is to be explained as a process of acquisition, the account must add an ordering of occurrences and specify how target-side and receiving-side occurrences are related. If retention is to be explained, it must also supply a criterion connecting the earlier and later bearer. Neither requirement follows from the mere existence of \(C\). The temporal critique makes precisely this distinction between rejecting a metric or universal time and acknowledging the ordinal structure already required by preservation and succession. ## 7. The foundation established so far

The commitments and their consequences can now be stated without advancing to later epistemic notions.

| Commitment | What it permits | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| **Differentiability of \(F\)** | A differentiation attributable to what is under consideration. | A particular articulation or its actual occurrence. |
| **A specified articulation** | Distinguishable terms and the relation under which they differ. | Reception, exhaustive partition, or objecthood. |
| **A differentiated receiving role \(R\)** | Distinguishable receiving conditions. | A knowing subject or a connection to \(F\). |
| **Receptive dependence \(C\)** | Structural registration of a target-side articulation. | Understanding, temporal acquisition, or retention. |

This is not a derivation of everything from differentiability. It establishes why further commitments become necessary and distinguishes those commitments from what they allow us to define. In particular, neither the receiving role nor its dependence on the target is concealed inside the initial claim about \(F\).

The foundational proposal is therefore that an elementary account of reception requires **differentiation and receptive dependence**, with target and recipient distinguished as roles in that dependence. Whether receptive dependence can itself be explained through more elementary relations remains a substantive question; replacing it with an unspecified arrow would not answer it.

The reconstruction stops at structural registration. Temporal continuity, memory, recognition, and representation have not been assumed in order to obtain that result, and none has yet been derived from it.
